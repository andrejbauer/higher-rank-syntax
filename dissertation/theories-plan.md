## 1. Signatures and their models

**Definition 1.1.** A *signature* is an object Ξ of Syn. Syn/Ξ is a model of ToS^ω (types, terms and
all operations of Def 3.1 are those of Syn; they are stable under substitution). Syn_Ξ is its
contextual core: objects Ξ.Θ, morphisms the σ : Ξ.Θ' → Ξ.Θ with p_Θ ∘ σ = p_Θ'. It is a sub-model.
Let wk : Syn → Syn_Ξ be the unique morphism (weakening), and δ := ⟨id, ν⟩ : Ξ → wk(Ξ) the diagonal,
the *tautological point*.

**Definition 1.2.** A *model of Ξ in* D ∈ Mod(ToS^ω) is a point M : ⊤ → F_D(Ξ).

**Proposition 1.3 (classifying model)** [proved]. G ↦ G(δ) is a bijection from morphisms
Syn_Ξ → D of models of ToS^ω to models of Ξ in D.

*Proof.* G ∘ wk = F_D by the black box, so G(δ) : ⊤ → F_D(Ξ). Existence: F_M(Ξ.Θ) := ⊤.F_D(Θ)[M],
F_M(A) := F_D(A)[M^{+Θ}], likewise on terms, and on morphisms by the universal property of the
pullback squares of lifts; F_M is a morphism because F_D is and all operations commute with
substitution; F_M(δ) = M. Uniqueness: !^{+Ξ} ∘ δ = id_Ξ, so Θ = Θ[!^{+Ξ}][δ], where Θ[!^{+Ξ}] is the
Θ-part of wk(Ξ.Θ); hence G(Ξ.Θ) = ⊤.F_D(Θ)[G δ], and likewise for types and terms. ∎

Instances: models of Ξ in Syn_Δ are the morphisms Δ → Ξ of Syn; in PSh(𝒞) (§2) they are the global
elements of the presheaf F(Ξ), Kaposi–Xie's naive semantics (Def 18) for arbitrary rank. Precedents:
Lawvere's functorial semantics; adjoining an indeterminate = slicing (Lambek–Scott functional
completeness) [cite, verify].

## 2. Theories, general and standard models

**Definition 2.1.** A *theory* T = (Ξ, 𝒫): a signature and a subpresheaf 𝒫 ⊆ Ty on Syn_Ξ of
*admissible* types (stable under substitution), usually generated by finitely many schemas
(Ξ.Δ_i, a_i). *Admissible chains*: telescopes with admissible entries. 𝒞_T ⊆ Syn_Ξ: the full
subcategory on admissible chains; with the admissible types it is a contextual natural model, the
*syntactic category* of T.

**Definition 2.2.** A *general model* of T: (D, M, 𝒞) with M a model of Ξ in D and 𝒞 ⊆ D a replete
full subcategory containing ⊤ and closed under admissible extensions: for I ∈ 𝒞, γ : I → F_M(Ξ.Θ)
and A ∈ 𝒫(Ξ.Θ), the object I.F_M(A)[γ] is in 𝒞. Morphisms: morphisms G : D → D' of models of ToS^ω
with G(M) = M', G(𝒞) ⊆ 𝒞'. A *standard model* is a general model with D = PSh(𝒩) for a small
category 𝒩 (Hofmann–Streicher universe, presheaf Π, equalisers [cite]) and 𝒞 the representables;
the closure condition then says that admissible types are locally representable.

**Theorem 2.3 (interpretation)** [proved]. (Syn_Ξ, δ, 𝒞_T) is the initial general model of T.
*Proof.* Proposition 1.3, and F_M(𝒞_T) ⊆ 𝒞 by induction on admissible chains. ∎

**Remark 2.4 (what this is not).** Theorem 2.3 holds for every theory and is the precise form of
Voevodsky's remark that term models are slices of LF's term model. It is not the initiality
conjecture for T: morphisms of general models are strict morphisms of the ambients (they preserve U
and every Π of the framework), so two standard models are essentially never connected (a monoid
homomorphism is not a morphism of general models), and the ambient Syn_Ξ of the initial object is
not standard. General models are to standard ones as Henkin models are to full ones. For
non-positive theories the syntax is not a standard model at all (§4.5).

## 3. Positive theories and first-order models

**Definition 3.1 (positivity)** [PT 3.2–3.3, restated for entries]. Over X ∈ Syn_Ξ, an entry [Θ]C
is *positive* if C is an element conclusion B or an element equation l =_B r, and every entry of Θ
is admissible and positive (over the extension by the preceding ones); it has *positive binders* if
every entry of Θ is positive. T is *positive* if every entry of Ξ has positive binders and every
admissible type is positive. So binders range over admissible types only, and never over `type` or
a sort equation.

**Lemma 3.2 (binding over an extension)** [PT 2.3]. In PSh(𝒩), if A over y(I) is represented by
(I ⊲ A, p, v), then Π(A, C)(I) ≅ C(I ⊲ A), b ↦ b(p, v).

**Definition 3.3 (model)** [PT 4.1]. For T positive: (𝒩, M, ⊲) with (PSh(𝒩), M) a standard model
and ⊲ chosen representing objects for admissible types at points of positive chains (stable under
substitution). *Contextual* if every object of 𝒩 is uniquely an iterated chosen extension of 1.
**Morphisms** [PT 5.2]: (F, φ), F : 𝒩 → 𝒩' preserving 1 and chosen extensions, φ a natural
transformation F_M ⇒ F* F_{M'} of functors on positive chains, compatible with the extensions.
This is Uemura's notion of morphism restricted to positive chains [cite, verify].

**Proposition 3.4** [to prove]. Mod(T) is the category of models of a finitary essentially
algebraic theory 𝕋_T extending 𝕋_NM: a presheaf over the fillings of its telescope for every sort
slot, an operation for every element slot, an axiom for every equational entry; fillings are
defined by recursion on positive chains using Lemma 3.2. Hence Mod(T) is locally finitely
presentable. Contextual models form a coreflective full subcategory. (PT 5.3 is one half.)

**Proposition 3.5 (adequacy)** [PT 6.2]. There is a model 𝒮_T = (𝒞_T, M_T, ⊲) with
Hom_{Syn_Ξ}(Θ, Δ) ≅ F_{M_T}(Δ)(Θ) naturally, for Θ admissible and Δ positive.

**Theorem 3.6 (initiality)**. For T positive, 𝒮_T is initial in Mod(T).
*Proof.* Existence: Theorem 2.3 at the standard model of 𝒩, restricted to 𝒞_T, with Proposition 3.5
to define φ [PT 7.3, the construction of J replaced by the black box]. Uniqueness: Yoneda at the
tautological points [PT 7.4]. ∎

**Corollary 3.7 (completeness).** For T positive, a judgement over an admissible chain with
positive conclusion is derivable iff it holds in all standard models.

Unfolded (contextual models): a GAT with 𝒫 = ∅ gives its Set-models (monoids: Mon); monoids with
El mon admissible: Lawvere theories under Th(Mon); untyped λ: λ-theories; MLTT: C-systems / CwFs
with the type formers; STLC: CwFs with arrow types whose types may depend on the context. CCCs:
see §6.1. No 2-categorical notion
is needed for initiality: out of a contextual model strict and pseudo morphisms agree
(Ahrens–Lumsdaine–North, Prop. 46) [cite].

## 4. Comparison with SOGATs

**4.1 Second-order ⊊ positive.** Ω ↦ (Ξ_Ω, 𝒫_Ω), 𝒫_Ω generated by El of the U⁺-sorts. Image: the
second-order theories (rank ≤ 3, binder entries element conclusions, 𝒫 = declared sorts;
`beyond-sogats-syntactic.md` 1.1). Standard models = KX naive semantics (Def 18); 𝕋_T = their GAT
translation (Construction 26), whose models are the not necessarily contextual ones [to prove].
Positive theories add: unbounded rank, admissible Π-types (contexts with metavariables), element
equations as hypotheses, a free choice of 𝒫.

**4.2 Positive theories are SOGAT-expressible up to Morita equivalence.** Morita equivalence :=
equivalence of categories of models. By 3.4 every positive theory is Morita equivalent to a GAT.
So a separation needs a non-positive theory.

**4.3 Homomorphisms** (rewritten in the fifth round, 2026-11-17). Let Ξ be a signature, nothing
admissible. Its models are the points of F_𝐒(Ξ), 𝐒 the model of ToS^ω in sets. Let 𝐅 be the model of
ToS^ω in spans of sets (presheaves on 1 → R ← 2) with U_R(A_1, A_2) := the functions A_1 → A_2 and
El(k)_R := the graph of k; Π and Id as in presheaves. The two projections 𝐅 → 𝐒 are morphisms of
models of ToS^ω.

**Definition.** A *homomorphism* M → M' is a model of Ξ in 𝐅 over (M, M'). Unfolded: functions h_S
between the sorts (families for dependent sorts) such that every operation of M is related to the
same operation of M', where: a ~ a' at a sort S iff h_S(a) = a'; f ~ f' at [Φ]c iff f(φ) ~ f'(φ')
whenever φ ~ φ'; bound sort variables Y, Y' are related by any function k, and then y ~ y' iff
k(y) = y'; an equational entry is related to itself when it holds on both sides. The definition has
no input besides Ξ.

(a) Ξ first-order: the usual homomorphisms; for T positive with 𝒫 = ∅, the morphisms of 3.3
[PT A.7].
(b) Isomorphisms: the same with U_R := bijections. They form a groupoid for every Ξ (transport of
structure) [routine, not written].
(c) Homomorphisms need not compose [checked]. Ξ = (X : type, c : [f : [x:X]X]X); X_1 = {0},
X_2 = {0,1}, X_3 = {0,1,2}; h, k the inclusions; c_1 = 0; c_2(f) = 0 if f(0) = 0, else 1;
c_3(f) = c_2(f|{0,1}) if f preserves {0,1}, else 2. Then h and k are homomorphisms and kh is not:
f = (0 ↦ 0, 1 ↦ 2, 2 ↦ 2) commutes with kh, and c_3(f) = 2. With bound sort variables the identity
can fail to be a homomorphism (a non-parametric operation).
(d) So a signature has models, isomorphisms and homomorphisms, and the homomorphisms may or may not
form a category. Say that Ξ *has a category of models* if identities are homomorphisms and
homomorphisms compose: a property, no data. First-order signatures have it; T_K has it (4.4). No
syntactic class is defined (7.3).
(e) Isomorphism classes cannot separate a theory from GATs: N with zero, succ and a recursor with
uniqueness has one model with trivial automorphisms, so its category of models is the terminal
category, as for the empty GAT. A separation up to Morita equivalence needs a category of models
that is not locally finitely presentable.

**4.4 Compact Hausdorff spaces.** Signature Ξ_K, 𝒫 = ∅, with a ∧ b := if(𝟚, b, ff, a),
¬a := if(𝟚, ff, tt, a):

    𝟚 : type,  tt : 𝟚,  ff : 𝟚,
    if : [B : type, a : B, b : B, x : 𝟚] B,
      [B, a, b] (if(B,a,b,tt) =_B a),   [B, a, b] (if(B,a,b,ff) =_B b),
      [B, g : [x:𝟚]B, x : 𝟚] (g(x) =_B if(B, g(tt), g(ff), x)),
    X : type,
    lim : [u : [p : [x:X]𝟚]𝟚,
           [p, q : [x:X]𝟚] (u([x] p(x) ∧ q(x)) =_𝟚 u(p) ∧ u(q)),
           [p : [x:X]𝟚] (u([x] ¬p(x)) =_𝟚 ¬u(p))] X,
      [x : X] (lim([p] p(x)) =_X x),
      [𝔘 : [P : [u, …]𝟚]𝟚, 𝔘 preserves ∧ and ¬]
          (lim([p] 𝔘([u] u(p))) =_X lim([p] 𝔘([u] p(lim(u))))).

lim has rank 4, the shape ((A → B) → C) ⇒ D that Uemura's thesis names as not writable in a SOGAT;
the last axiom has rank 6. The equational premises are derivable where lim is applied (checked).

**Theorem 4.4.** The Set-models of T_K, with functional logical relations as morphisms, form the
category KHaus of compact Hausdorff spaces. KHaus is not locally presentable. Hence T_K is not
Morita equivalent to any positive theory, GAT or SOGAT (finitary or infinitary).
*Proof.* In Set the eliminator forces 𝟚 ≅ 2; the premises of lim cut out the Boolean homomorphisms
2^X → 2, i.e. βX; the two axioms are the Eilenberg–Moore laws; so models are β-algebras, which are
the compact Hausdorff spaces (Manes 1969). A functional logical relation h relates u₁ to u₂ iff
u₂ = β(h)(u₁), so the morphisms are the β-homomorphisms, the continuous maps. If KHaus were locally
presentable, the free algebra on one generator would be μ-presentable for a regular μ, so
β = UF would preserve μ-directed colimits; but for |S| = μ, a uniform ultrafilter on S lies in no
β(S') with |S'| < μ. Models of positive theories are l.f.p. (3.4). ∎
Remarks (fifth round). (i) The proof uses no notion of variance. The mechanism: `if` with its
uniqueness axiom forces h to be the bijection matching tt and ff on 𝟚; then p ~ p' iff p = p' ∘ h,
and u ~ u' iff u' = β(h)(u). (ii) Without the two premises of lim the models are the algebras of
the double power set monad, i.e. complete atomic Boolean algebras, a category equivalent to Set^op
and also not locally presentable [classical; citation to find and verify]. (iii) Withdrawn:
suplattices, frames and complete Boolean algebras as "the same family". For sup : [p : [x:X]𝟚]X a
homomorphism is an h with h(sup h⁻¹B) = sup' B for all B ⊆ X'; these compose but are not the
sup-preserving maps: the parity map from the chain 0<1<2<3 onto 0<1 is one and is not monotone
[checked]. The examples of this kind are the equationally defined submonads of X ↦ 2^(2^X).
(iv) The separation concerns models in sets, where a binder ranges over all functions; compare
second-order logic with full semantics.

**4.5 Incompleteness.** N with zero, succ and a recursor into arbitrary sorts with uniqueness has,
in every standard model, the natural numbers object; all true Π⁰₁ equations hold there, while
derivability is r.e. So Corollary 3.7 fails without positivity, and the syntax of a non-positive
theory is in general not a standard model.

## 6. Addenda (2026-11-16, second round)

**6.1 CCCs.** (a) The first-order theory of CCCs (Ob, Hom, identities, composition, terminal object,
products, exponentials with evaluation and currying; rank 2, nothing admissible) has as contextual
models exactly the CCCs with chosen structure and strict functors. (b) Castellan–Clairambault–Dybjer,
arXiv 1904.00827, Theorem 5 [verified from the PDF]: the category of CCCs with structure and strict
functors is *equivalent* to the category of small contextual simply typed cwfs with N1-, ×- and
⇒-structure and strict morphisms (their version of Lambek–Scott Thm 3.11); Theorem 3 is the same
without ⇒, for cartesian categories; Theorem 4 is the biequivalence for democratic scwfs. (c) The
contextual models of the STLC signature with unit, products and arrows are contextual cwfs with these
type formers in which types may depend on the context. Adding strengthening,

    str : [A : ty, B : [x : tm(A)] ty] ty,
      [A, C : ty] (str(A, [x] C) =_ty C),   [A, B, x : tm(A)] (B(x) =_ty str(A, [x'] B(x'))),

makes weakening Ty(Γ) → Ty(Γ.A) bijective, so the contextual models are the simply typed ones, hence
equivalent to CCCs by (b) [checked by hand]. So (a) and (c) are two Morita equivalent positive
presentations.

**6.2 The essentially algebraic theory 𝕋_T.** Sorts Con, Sub with a terminal object; for every sort
slot y : [Θ]type a sort y(Γ, θ) over contexts Γ and fillings θ of Θ, with a substitution action; for
every element slot an operation natural in Γ; for every equational entry an axiom; for every
admissible schema a comprehension structure. A filling assigns to an entry z : [Φ]B an element of B
over Γ.Φ, which exists as a context because Φ is admissible, and to an equational entry the
requirement that it holds over Γ.Φ. For MLTT this is Dybjer's GAT of cwfs with the type formers. For
second-order T it is Kaposi–Xie's Construction 26 (Thms 22, 27). All models of T = Set-models of the
finite GAT 𝕋_T; contextual models = Set-models of a GAT with one sort of contexts per length.
Converse: a finite GAT is a positive theory of rank 2 with nothing admissible. So, up to Morita
equivalence, positive theories are the finitary essentially algebraic theories; positivity itself is
a property of presentations (a binder over a sort forced to be finite is harmless).

**6.3 Covariance** [withdrawn in the fifth round, see 4.3 and 7.3; the examples listed at the
end are wrong for suplattices]. Polarity of types relative to rigid sorts (sorts on which every
morphism is bijective, e.g. 𝟚): El S is covariant; a rigid El S is also contravariant; [Θ]c is
covariant if the entries of Θ are contravariant and c covariant, and contravariant if the entries
are covariant and c contravariant. T is covariant if every entry of every slot telescope is
covariant. Then a functional logical relation is a family of functions h commuting with the
operations along the covariant actions, and these compose [simply typed case checked; dependent
case to do]. Arity of an operation = size of its binder domains: rigid finite ⇒ finitary
(GAT-equivalent); rigid infinite, e.g. N with a recursor ⇒ infinitary bounded (ω-cpos, σ-algebras;
locally ℵ₁-presentable [from memory]); the carrier itself ⇒ unbounded (KHaus, suplattices).
Syntax not initial: T_K with N, zero, succ and emb : [n : N] X has initial model βℕ, while its
closed terms of sort X are the emb(n).

**6.4 Contextual models or all.** Recommendation: Mod(T) := contextual models; initiality also
stated for all models (same proof, or by the coreflection onto contextual cores). Reasons: GATs get
their usual Set-models, so one semantics covers all ranks; strict morphisms are legitimate (strict
and pseudo agree out of contextual models), so no 2-categories; Voevodsky's formulation; CCCs and
Lawvere theories come out on the nose (6.1); Chapter 2 already has contextual natural models and
cores. Cost: semantic universes (Set, presheaves) are not objects of Mod(T); they are standard
models, each with a contextual core.

## 7. Addenda (2026-11-16, third round)

**7.1 The classifying category of a positive theory.** For T positive let 𝒫os_T ⊆ Syn_Ξ be the full
subcategory on the positive chains, with the positive entries as types. It is a contextual natural
model, closed under substitution, with an equation type for every pair of terms of a slot type,
hence with finite limits. Let 𝔽am be the natural model of sets and families.

**Theorem 7.1** [to prove]. For a contextual model (𝒩, M, ⊲), G_M := ev_1 ∘ F_M restricted to 𝒫os_T
is a morphism of natural models 𝒫os_T → 𝔽am, and this is an equivalence from the contextual models
of T to CxlNM(𝒫os_T, 𝔽am), with natural transformations as morphisms.
*Sketch.* ev_1 : PSh(𝒩) → 𝔽am preserves context extension (not Π). By Lemma 3.2,
F_M(Θ')(1 ⊲ Δ[γ]) is the fibre of G_M(Θ.[Δ]Θ') over γ, so G_M determines M when 𝒩 is contextual.
Conversely, from G build 𝒩_G: objects (Θ, γ ∈ G(Θ), Δ an admissible chain over Θ) modulo reindexing
(a coend); morphisms (Θ, γ, Δ') → (Θ, γ, Δ) the fibre of G(Θ.[Δ']Δ) over γ; composition from the
substitution terms of 𝒫os_T. Detail: ⊲ must be indexed by points of admissible schemas up to
reindexing, not by semantic types.

So the GAT of T, for contextual models, has the positive entries as sorts and the positive chains
as contexts. It replaces 𝕋_T of 6.2, which is the version for all models. Instances:
(S : type; El S admissible) gives abstract clones ≃ Lawvere theories. For MLTT the positive entries
[Δ]ty and [Δ]tm(A) are the sorts of B-systems, and 7.1 is B-systems ≃ C-systems with the type
formers (Ahrens–Emmenegger–North–Rijke, Algebraic presentations of type dependency, LMCS 21(1),
2025) [verified]. First-order T with 𝒫 = ∅: Cartmell's syntactic category with equation types
added. Precedent: Uemura, arXiv 1904.04097: the theories over a type theory are bi-equivalent to a
full sub-2-category of its models [abstract verified; that it is the democratic models is from
memory].

**Corollary 7.2.** Under 7.1 the syntactic model 𝒮_T is the functor 𝒫os_T(⊤, −), and it is initial
by the Yoneda lemma, since G(⊤) = 1. An alternative proof of Theorem 3.6.

**7.2 Comparison with GATs: the statements** (rewritten in the fourth round; the earlier version
claimed more than is true).

**The correspondence** (fifth round: this is what the chapter claims; morphisms of theories are not
pursued, the user: "perhaps that's not that important").
(→) A GAT U is the positive theory (Ξ_U, ∅). Same syntax; contextual models = Set-models of U.
(←) A positive theory T has the GAT 𝕋_T of 6.2: contexts of admissible types and substitutions;
every sort and operation of T in every context; a binder z : [Φ]B becomes an argument of sort B in
the context extended by Φ. In the contextual version contexts are graded by length, so 𝕋_T may have
countably many sorts (untyped λ: Tm(n), var, sub, lam : Tm(n+1) → Tm(n), app). Then
Mod(T) ≃ Mod(𝕋_T) [Prop 3.4, to prove in general; an unfolding by Lemma 3.2].
Round trips: 𝕋_{(Ξ_U, ∅)} is U; (𝕋_T, ∅) is a first-order theory with the same models as T. So the
translations are mutually inverse up to Morita equivalence: positive theories add binding, not
models. Theorem 7.1 presents the same GAT for contextual models by its syntactic category
(sorts = positive entries); it is optional.

Classical side. GAT: Cartmell's category of generalised algebraic theories and interpretations (a
sort symbol goes to a sort expression, an operator symbol to a term, modulo provable equality).
Con: contextual categories. C : GAT → Con, U ↦ its syntactic category, is an equivalence, and
Mod(U) = Con(C(U), 𝔽am) [Cartmell 1986; seen only through secondary sources]. Lex: small finitely
complete categories. Gabriel–Ulmer: 𝒜 ↦ Lex(𝒜, Set) is an equivalence Lex^op ≃ LFP.

**Claim 1 (object level)** = Theorem 7.1. For T positive, Mod(T) ≃ Con(𝒫os_T, 𝔽am) ≃
Lex(𝒫os_T, Set). So T has the same models as the GAT 𝔾_T with C(𝔾_T) = 𝒫os_T: sorts are the
positive entries, contexts the positive chains, terms the expressions of T, equations the derivable
ones. Equivalently, 𝒫os_T ≃ (finitely presentable models of T)^op.
*Supporting proposition* [from PT 6.2 and 7.5]. For a positive chain Θ, T[Θ] := (Ξ.Θ, 𝒫) is positive.
A model of T[Θ] is a model M of T with an element of F_M(Θ)(1). So the syntactic model of T[Θ]
represents M ↦ F_M(Θ)(1), and Θ ↦ 𝒮_{T[Θ]} is a full embedding 𝒫os_T^op → Mod(T). A positive chain is
a finite presentation of a model. Missing for 7.1: a contextual model is determined by these sets.
*Converse.* A finite GAT U is the positive theory (Ξ_U, ∅), and its contextual models are the
Set-models of U.

**Claim 2 (morphism level)** [not pursued, fifth round] has three readings.
(2a) Syntactic morphisms, first order [to prove: adequacy]. Let Sig_1 ⊆ Syn be the full subcategory
on the first-order signatures with variable binders only. Then Sig_1^op is Cartmell's category of
finite GATs: a morphism Ξ' → Ξ of Syn is an interpretation of Ξ in Ξ'.
(2b) Syntactic morphisms, positive theories [not an equivalence]. Morphisms T → T' are the
f : Ξ' → Ξ of Syn with f*𝒫 ⊆ 𝒫'. Then T ↦ 𝒫os_T, f ↦ f* is a functor to Con. It is not full: for
T = (S : type; El S admissible), prefixing a variable y : S to every binder is a contextual
endofunctor of 𝒫os_T (on models, L ↦ L[y]) induced by no signature morphism.
(2c) Semantic morphisms [formal consequence of Claim 1 and its converse]. Morphisms T → T' are the
functors Mod(T') → Mod(T) preserving limits and filtered colimits; by Gabriel–Ulmer these are the
lex functors 𝒫os_T → 𝒫os_T'. With them positive theories and GATs form equivalent 2-categories
exactly when they present the same categories of models. With infinite signatures both present
all locally finitely presentable categories.

Correction. An earlier version identified, for first-order theories, contextual functors between
the categories of positive chains with signature morphisms. That is false: a contextual functor may
send a sort to an equation type, and a lex functor may send it to a chain.

Finiteness. For T = (S : type; El S admissible), Mod(T) = Law is not the category of models of any
GAT with finitely many sorts. Such a category has a finite strong generator of finitely presentable
objects. In Law, for each N the map from the theory of pointed sets to the theory with a constant
c and an (N+1)-ary t, with t = c on tuples containing c or a repetition, is bijective on operations
of arity ≤ N and is not an isomorphism [checked]. So finite positive theories present strictly
more than finite GATs and at most countable GATs.

"Nothing beyond GATs", precisely. Up to equivalence of categories,
{Mod(U) : U a finite GAT} ⊊ {Mod(T) : T positive} ⊊ {locally finitely presentable categories}.
Right inclusion: Claim 1, with Adámek–Rosický Thm 3.36 (l.f.p. = models of a finitary essentially
algebraic theory). Left inclusion: the converse. Left strict: Law, above. Right strict: Syn is
countable, so Mod(T) has countably many finitely presentable objects with countable hom-sets; real
vector spaces are excluded. With infinite signatures the right inclusion is an equality. The
statement concerns categories of models only, not syntax and not morphisms of theories.

Admissibility. Claim 1 forgets 𝒫. Morphisms as in (2b) remember it; they are of the kind of Uemura's
morphisms of representable map categories. With those, positive theories should correspond to

What survives, as a tool and not as a definition [proved on paper in the fourth round with
identities on the backward sorts, dependent sorts included, no bound sort variables; the bijective
case is the same argument, to recheck]. Let h : M → M' be a homomorphism. Call an entry *forward*
if its relation along every homomorphism is the graph of a function from the M-side to the M'-side,
*backward* if it is the graph of a function from the M'-side to the M-side. Every sort S(t) and
every element equation is forward. A sort is backward iff every homomorphism is bijective on it;
this is a property of the theory (it holds for 𝟚 in T_K because of `if`), not data. [Φ]c is
forward if the entries of Φ are backward and c is forward, with action f ↦ c(h) ∘ f ∘ Φ(h); it is
backward if the entries of Φ are forward and c is backward. If every binder entry of every slot is
forward or backward, the actions are functorial and homomorphisms compose. Not of this kind:
c : [f : [x:X]X]X (4.3 (c)).
