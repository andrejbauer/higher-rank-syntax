# Equations over raw higher-rank syntax

This note proposes an equation layer for the existing raw syntax.  The basic
idea is to keep raw expressions unchanged, declare equation schemas between
them, and generate the least structural and substitution-stable congruence
containing those schemas.  Ordinary set quotients are then a derived
construction.

This avoids making the whole syntax development setoid-valued.  Raw
expressions remain available for recursion and induction, while the quotient
provides the extensional syntax in which the declared equations hold as
ordinary equality.

## 1. Equations belong to a presentation

Fix a carrier `C` and a signature arity `S`.  The signature is kept as a fixed
prefix, so the raw terms of the theory have the form

```text
STerm S Γ Φ τ = Expr (S ⋈ Γ ⋈ Φ) τ.
```

The three parts of the ambient arity have different roles:

- `S` contains the protected operation symbols of the theory;
- `Γ` is the metavariable context of an equation schema;
- `Φ` contains local bound variables that remain fixed during an
  instantiation;
- `τ` is the existing coarse class of the expression.

An equational presentation over `S` is an indexed family of pairs of such
terms.  Schematically, its main field is

```lean
axiom :
  ∀ {Γ Φ τ},
    STerm S Γ Φ τ →
    STerm S Γ Φ τ →
    Prop
```

Thus `axiom e e'` says that `e = e'` is one of the declared equation schemas.
The slots in `Γ` act as metavariables: substituting arbitrary expressions for
them produces instances of the schema.  Higher-rank slots in `Γ` can express
schemas with metavariables that themselves take arguments.

The equations should not be placed in `Carrier` or built into `Expr`.  A
carrier determines the general shape of raw syntax, and a signature determines
its operation symbols.  Equations are an additional part of a particular
theory presentation:

```text
equational theory = signature + equation schemas.
```

## 2. The generated equality judgment

For a presentation `E`, define a proof-irrelevant proposition

```text
DerivEq E e e'.
```

It is generated by the declared axioms and the standard equality rules:

```text
axiom      E declares e = e'
           -----------------
                e ≈ e'

refl            e ≈ e

symm            e ≈ e'
           -----------------
                e' ≈ e

trans           e ≈ e'    e' ≈ e''
           ------------------------
                       e ≈ e''
```

It is also a structural congruence.  Applications with the same head are
related whenever all corresponding arguments are related:

```text
for every argument position i, args(i) ≈ args'(i)
--------------------------------------------------
             ap x args ≈ ap x args'.
```

For a higher-rank head, an argument may live in a context extended by variables
bound at that argument position.  The premise for that argument is therefore
formed in precisely that extended context.  The existing `Expr.Args` family
already records these contexts.

### Two-sided substitution

The central closure rule changes the expression and its substitution at the
same time:

```text
e ≈ e'

for every slot x, σ(x) ≈ θ(x)
--------------------------------
          e[σ] ≈ e'[θ].
```

In terms of the raw operations, the conclusion is built using `Subst.act` with
the fixed prefix `S`.  This rule has two important special cases.

Taking `θ = σ` and using reflexivity for all fillers gives stability of
equations under substitution:

```text
e ≈ e'  implies  e[σ] ≈ e'[σ].
```

Taking `e' = e` gives congruence in the substitution:

```text
if σ(x) ≈ θ(x) for every x, then e[σ] ≈ e[θ].
```

The second property will make quotient substitution independent of the chosen
representatives of its fillers.  Renaming stability follows as a special case,
because a renaming acts by substitution with eta-expanded slots.

Consequently, `DerivEq E` is the least equivalence relation that contains the
declared schemas, respects the raw expression constructors, and is compatible
with substitution in both inputs.

## 3. The monoid presentation

The first example should extend the magma signature by a nullary unit symbol:

```text
e   : term
mul : term × term → term.
```

Associativity is a schema in a context with three ordinary metavariables:

```text
Γ₃ = (x, y, z)

mul (mul x y) z = mul x (mul y z).
```

The two unit schemas live in a context with one metavariable:

```text
Γ₁ = (x)

mul e x = x
mul x e = x.
```

The schemas themselves mention only variables.  Two-sided substitution
produces their arbitrary instances.  For expressions `p`, `q`, and `r`, it
gives

```text
mul (mul p q) r ≈ mul p (mul q r).
```

Structural congruence gives, for example,

```text
p ≈ p'    q ≈ q'
------------------
mul p q ≈ mul p' q'.
```

The monoid example therefore tests the three essential parts of the generic
definition: metavariable schemas, structural congruence, and simultaneous
substitution.

## 4. Quotient expressions

The constructors of `DerivEq` make it an equivalence relation.  We may
therefore define the quotient set

```text
QExpr E Γ Φ τ = STerm S Γ Φ τ / DerivEq E.
```

In Lean this uses a locally bundled `Setoid`, because that is the input expected
by `Quotient`.  It does not require the surrounding framework to become a
category of setoids.

Write `[e]` for the equivalence class of a raw expression.  Every derivable
equation now becomes ordinary equality:

```text
e ≈ e'  implies  [e] = [e'].
```

In the monoid quotient, in particular,

```text
[(p · q) · r] = [p · (q · r)]
[e · p]       = [p]
[p · e]       = [p].
```

The expected concrete description is that monoid terms in `n` variables,
modulo these equations, are finite lists of elements of `Fin n`.

## 5. The quotient relative monad

The quotient syntax should again form a fixed-prefix relative monad, with
object map

```text
T_E Γ (Φ, τ) = QExpr E Γ Φ τ.
```

The source functor remains the existing set-valued slots functor:

```text
J Γ (Φ, τ) = Γ ∋[τ] Φ.
```

Thus slots acquire no equation relation and do not become setoids.  The unit is

```text
x ↦ [Expr.η x].
```

Given a quotient-valued substitution, choose a raw representative for every
filler and set

```text
[e] ↦ [e[σ]].
```

Two-sided substitution congruence proves that the result is independent both
of the representative of `e` and of all chosen filler representatives.  For a
completely arbitrary carrier, simultaneous choice of those representatives
may use classical choice.  This issue can be isolated inside the construction
of the quotient Kleisli extension; it does not affect the raw equation layer.

The raw quotient map

```text
q : T'_S → T_E,
q(e) = [e],
```

should be a morphism of relative monads.  Its universal property says that any
substitution-compatible interpretation of raw syntax that makes every axiom of
`E` true factors uniquely through `T_E`.  This is the categorical content of
imposing the equations.

## 6. Decorations, typed equality, and conversion

The raw equation layer does not replace decorations.  A decoration still
stores a raw classifier expression: in the declaration `x : A`, it stores the
expression `A` as the boundary of `x`.

Later, two classifiers can be related by applying `DerivEq` to their expression
components.  At the judgmental layer, conversion would have the form

```text
HasType Γ t A    A ≈ B
----------------------
     HasType Γ t B.
```

The raw term in the conclusion is exactly the same `t`.  Alternatively, after
quotienting type expressions, typing can be indexed by the class `[A]`.
Because `A ≈ B` gives `[A] = [B]`, conversion is then ordinary transport along
equality of quotient types.

For a genuinely dependent theory, raw congruence is not yet the whole story.
Equality should eventually be restricted to well-formed judgments such as

```text
Γ ⊢ A ≡ B type
Γ ⊢ s ≡ t : A.
```

In particular, term equality should assert a common dependent classifier, and
type equality should be formed only in a well-formed context.  These conditions
belong to the later judgment layer.  The raw congruence is already sufficient
for untyped or coarsely typed algebraic examples such as monoids, and supplies
the structural substitution machinery needed by the dependent refinement.

## 7. Equation judgments are not equation-valued expressions

An expression of a coarse class such as `.equation` is syntax representing an
equation derivation.  Its mere existence does not identify its two endpoints.
In particular, adding a constructor

```text
equation : Expr → Expr → Expr
```

would only produce more raw syntax.

By contrast, `DerivEq E e e' : Prop` is an external judgment relating two raw
expressions.  It supports quotienting and therefore makes the endpoints equal
in the extensional syntax.  If proof-relevant equality derivations are wanted,
they can be defined separately and mapped into this proof-irrelevant relation.
