# Sorts, boundaries, equations, decorations

A definitional account, taking the raw relative monad as given.

**Revision.** An earlier draft built a sort signature `(rank, ∂)` on top of the
carrier's coarse classes `C.Ty`. That was wrong twice over: `C.Ty` and the set
of sort symbols were two names for one thing, and fixing `C.Ty` in the carrier
made the carrier commit in advance to the judgment forms that a theory is
supposed to *declare*. This draft removes `C.Ty` from the carrier. Sorts become
ordinary entries of a theory, and the consequences are large enough to be worth
stating up front:

- the separate notion of *sort signature* disappears — a sort's argument
  telescope is just its arity, like any other entry's;
- the ranking of sorts disappears — the declaration order already ranks them;
- a boundary is an **expression**;
- there is one equality form, parameterized by a sort expression, rather than
  one per sort;
- **no definition below is mutually recursive.** The rank recursion of the
  earlier draft was an artifact of separating `∂` from the decoration.

Nothing here asserts well-formedness. Every object below is annotation over raw
syntax; the judgments that assert things are T2 and are not treated here.

---

## 0. The starting point

**0.1 Definition (carrier).** A *carrier* `C` consists of a monoid `𝔸` of
**arities** (written `Γ ⋈ Δ`, unit `1`) and, for each `Γ, Δ ∈ 𝔸`, a well-ordered
set of **slots** `Γ ∋ Δ`, subject to: `1` has no slots, the slots of `Γ ⋈ Δ` are
the ordered sum of those of `Γ` and of `Δ`, and the relation "`Δ` is the arity
of some slot of `Γ`" is well founded.

A slot `x : Γ ∋ Δ` is read: *`Γ` has an entry `x` binding the telescope `Δ`.*
The arity `Δ` is an arity of the same monoid — this is what "higher-rank" means,
and the well-foundedness is what makes recursion on it legitimate.

The carrier is the ambient universe of available shapes and nothing more. It
knows about binding and about order. It knows nothing about types, sorts,
classes or equations.

**0.2 Definition (raw syntax).** `Expr Γ` is generated by the single rule

```
      x : Γ ∋ Δ          args :  ∀ ⦃Λ⦄, Δ ∋ Λ → Expr (Γ ⋈ Λ)
     ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                        ap x args : Expr Γ
```

A head is a slot of the ambient arity; it has one argument per slot of its own
arity, and that argument is written in the ambient arity extended by *that
slot's* binders. There is one syntactic category.

**0.3 Definition (the raw substitution category).** Let `𝕊 = 𝕊(C)` have arities
as objects and

```
𝕊(Δ, Ω)  :=  ∀ ⦃Λ⦄, Δ ∋ Λ → Expr (Ω ⋈ Λ)
```

with identity the η-expansion of each slot and composition capture-avoiding
substitution. `𝕊` is the Kleisli category of the relative monad
`T Ω Λ = Expr (Ω ⋈ Λ)` over the slot functor `J Ω Λ = Ω ∋ Λ`.

**0.4 Reading.** *An arity is a formal telescope of metavariables; `𝕊(Δ, Ω)` is
the set of instantiations of `Δ` by expressions over `Ω`.* Every construction
below is an instance of this one, and every action on it is composition in `𝕊`.

**0.5 Lemma (generic element).** Assume the carrier has an arity `∗̂` with
exactly one slot, of arity `1`. Then

```
𝕊(1, Ω) ≅ ∗                𝕊(∗̂, Ω) ≅ Expr Ω.
```

*An expression over `Ω` is the same thing as a map `∗̂ ⟶ Ω` in `𝕊`.* We use this
to speak of expressions and of tuples of expressions in one language.

**0.6 Standing hypothesis.** Every arity named below lies in `𝔸`. A theory may
only use shapes its carrier provides; this is a genuine constraint, not
bookkeeping.

## 1. Boundaries

**1.1 Motivation.** In the raw layer a slot carries no information beyond its
arity. Something must say what an entry *is*: a term of type `A`, a morphism
from `x` to `y`, a type. Cartmell's answer, for generalized algebraic theories,
is that a signature declares **sort symbols** alongside operation symbols, each
with a telescope of arguments,

```
s(z₁ : S₁, …, zₙ : Sₙ) sort
```

and that the type of a term is never a bare class but always an *instance*
`s(a₁,…,aₙ)`. Two conditions accompany this: the telescope is **dependent**, and
symbols are declared in **sequence**, so each mentions only earlier ones.

Both conditions are already met by a decorated telescope: the arity of a slot is
its argument telescope, and precedence is the declaration order. **So a sort
symbol needs no separate apparatus — it is an entry of the theory whose arity is
its argument telescope.** Its instances `s(a⃗)` are then ordinary expressions
`ap s a⃗`, and a boundary is an expression.

**1.2 Definition (boundary).** A **boundary over `Ω`** is an element of

```
Bd(Ω)  :=  Option (Expr Ω) .
```

`none` declares the entry to be a **sort**. `some S` declares it to be an
**object of sort `S`**, where `S` is a sort expression over `Ω`.

**1.3 Remark (this is the whole of the sort signature).** There is no `C.Ty`, no
`rank`, no `∂`. For an entry `s` declared with boundary `none`, the telescope
called `∂s` in the earlier draft is simply the arity of `s`. The ranking of
sorts is the precedence order on the theory, which exists already.

Consequently there is also no mutual recursion: `Expr` is defined from the
carrier alone, and boundaries are valued in `Expr`. The self-reference is
carried entirely by `before(x)` (Definition 2.1), which the existing decoration
machinery already handles.

**1.4 Proposition (the structure that was wanted).**

1. **Substitution acts by postcomposition.** `θ ∈ 𝕊(Ω, Ω')` transports
   `some S` to `some (θ ∘ S)` and fixes `none` — using 0.5, ordinary
   substitution into the sort expression.
2. **Instantiating a boundary by arguments is composition.** If a declaration
   with arity `Δ` has boundary `some S` with `S ∈ Expr (Ω ⋈ Δ)`, and is applied
   to `σ ∈ 𝕊(Δ, Ω)`, the boundary of the result is `some (σ ∘ S)`.
3. **The boundary former is a coproduct of representables.**
   `Bd = 1 + 𝕊(∗̂, −)`. It is a polynomial functor, so its action, binder
   lifting and positivity are determined by `∗̂`; its well-foundedness is that of
   `Expr`. The sort-indexed coproduct that an external signature would have
   supplied is internalized in `Expr`, where an application `ap s a⃗` already
   records which sort symbol and which arguments — which is where the polynomial
   structure belonged.

**1.5 Remark (raw boundaries are permissive).** Nothing requires the expression
`S` in `some S` to be headed by a sort entry, or the arguments to match that
entry's arity. `Bd(Ω)` is all of `Option (Expr Ω)`. Checking `Γ ⊢ S sort` — that
`S = ap s a⃗` with `s` a sort entry and `a⃗` well formed at `s`'s arity — is a T2
obligation, uniform with every other T2 obligation. This is the discipline of
2.4 and is what keeps the layer raw.

**1.6 Examples.**

| theory | sort entries | a typical object entry |
|---|---|---|
| unsorted | none | every entry has boundary `some ⋆` for one fixed sort `⋆`, or the boundary is inert |
| MLTT | `ty` with arity `1`; `tm` with arity `(A : ty)` | `Σ`, with boundary `some (ap ty ())` |
| categories | `obj` with arity `1`; `hom` with arity `(x : obj, y : obj)` | `comp`, with boundary `some (Hom x z)` |

Read the MLTT line: the entry `tm` is a sort declaration whose single argument
slot `A` has boundary `some (Ty)`. So `Tm A` is the expression `ap tm (A)`, and
the boundary of a term entry is `some (ap tm (A))`.

## 2. Decorations

**2.1 Definition (precedence).** A *precedence* on `C` assigns to each slot
`x : Δ ∋ Λ` a factorization `Δ = before(x) ⋈ after(x)` with `x` located in the
right factor, coherently with the injections. `before(x)` is the part of `Δ`
declared strictly before `x`.

**2.2 Definition (decorated telescope).** Let `Ω` be a base and `Δ` an arity. A
**decoration of `Δ` over `Ω`** assigns to every slot `x` of `Δ`, and recursively
to every slot nested inside the arity of a slot of `Δ`, a boundary

```
bnd(x)  ∈  Bd( Ω ⋈ before(x) ⋈ Λ_x )
```

where `Λ_x` is the arity of `x`. A **decorated telescope over `Ω`** is a pair
`Δ = (|Δ|, D)`; its **erasure** is `|Δ|`.

**2.3 Reading.** The three-part codomain is forced, and each part is used:

- `Ω` — the ambient theory, so a boundary may name declared sorts and symbols;
- `before(x)` — the earlier siblings, which is *dependency*: in
  `(A : Ty, a : Tm A)`, the boundary of `a` names `A`;
- `Λ_x` — the slot's own bound variables, which is *self-reference*: the
  boundary of `app(A, B, f, u)` is `Tm (B û)`, naming `app`'s own argument `û`.

Sort entries use the same three parts: `tm`'s argument slot `A` has boundary
`Ty`, which names the entry `ty` living in `Ω`.

**2.4 Remark (a decoration asserts nothing).** It records *which* boundary each
slot has. It does not say that boundary is a well-formed sort, that the slot
inhabits it, or that the telescope is a valid context.

**2.5 Proposition (decorations are a module over raw syntax).** `Ω ↦ DTel(Ω)` is
a functor `𝕊 → Set`: a substitution `θ ∈ 𝕊(Ω, Ω')` acts by postcomposing every
`bnd(x)`, leaving `|Δ|` fixed. The functor laws are the monad laws of `T`.

**2.6 Proposition (decorated telescopes form a monoid).** There is an empty
decorated telescope, and decorated telescopes concatenate dependently: given `Δ`
over `Ω` and `Ξ` over `Ω ⋈ |Δ|`, their concatenation `Δ · Ξ` is a decorated
telescope over `Ω` whose right half may name the left half. These are the unit
and multiplication of a monoid for the context-extension tensor on arity-shaped
`T`-modules.

**2.7 Definition (theory).** A *raw theory* is a decorated telescope over the
base `1`. Its slots are the declared symbols — sorts where `bnd` is `none`,
operations where it is `some S`. The precedence is the declaration order; the
decoration nested inside a symbol's arity gives the boundaries of its arguments.

**2.8 Example (Martin-Löf dependent sums).** Written as one decorated telescope
over `1`, in order:

```
ty   :  [ ]                                                  sort
tm   :  [ A : Ty ]                                           sort
Σ    :  [ A : Ty, B : [x : Tm A] Ty ]                        Ty
pair :  [ A : Ty, B : [x : Tm A] Ty, a : Tm A, b : Tm (B a) ]  Tm (Σ A B)
fst  :  [ A : Ty, B : [x : Tm A] Ty, p : Tm (Σ A B) ]        Tm A
snd  :  [ A : Ty, B : [x : Tm A] Ty, p : Tm (Σ A B) ]        Tm (B (fst A B p))
```

Here `Ty` abbreviates `ap ty ()` and `Tm E` abbreviates `ap tm (E)`; the first
two lines have boundary `none` and the rest `some (…)`.

Read `snd`: a slot of the theory whose arity is `(A, B, p)`. Inside that arity,
`p` has boundary `Tm (Σ A B)`, naming its earlier siblings — 2.3's `before(x)`.
The slot `B` has arity `[x]`, and the nested decoration gives `x` the boundary
`Tm A` — again `before(x)`, one level down. `snd`'s own boundary
`Tm (B (fst A B p))` is written over `1 ⋈ before(snd) ⋈ (A,B,p)`, using both the
earlier symbols `tm, Σ, fst` and its own arguments — 2.3's `Λ_x`.

Every expression above is an ordinary element of `Expr`, including the sort
expressions. Nothing asserts any of them is well formed.

## 3. Equations

**3.1 What an equation must be.** A slot *adds an inhabitant*; an equation
*removes a distinction*. In the presentation of a model these are the two
opposite operations — free extension by a generator, and coequalizer of a
parallel pair. So an equation cannot be a slot: a slot at an "equality sort"
would produce proof-relevant equality expressions and would still leave the two
sides distinct.

**3.2 Definition (equality boundary).** An **equality boundary over `Ω`** is a
triple

```
(S, e, e')   with   S, e, e' ∈ Expr Ω,
```

read *`e` and `e'` are equal at sort `S`*. Equivalently, by 0.5, a sort
expression together with a **parallel pair** `e, e' : ∗̂ ⇉ Ω` in `𝕊`.

There is one equality form, parameterized by a sort *expression*. It is not a
sort and not a member of any signature; it may appear only as the boundary of a
declaration, never as the boundary of a slot, and therefore never becomes a head
of `Expr`. Both MLTT equality judgments are instances: `S := Ty` gives
`A = B type`, and `S := Tm A` gives `u = v : A`.

**3.3 Definition (equation declaration).** An *equation declaration* over `Ω` is

```
q : [Θ] (e ≡ e' : S)
```

where `Θ` is a decorated telescope over `Ω` and `(S, e, e')` is an equality
boundary over `Ω ⋈ |Θ|`. The **content** of the declaration is the parallel pair
`e, e' : ∗̂ ⇉ Ω ⋈ |Θ|`, schematic in the slots of `Θ`, which are its
metavariables. The component `S` is annotation, and carries no further content.

**3.4 Definition (equational presentation).** An *equational presentation* over
`Ω` is a set `E` of equation declarations over `Ω`, each **positioned** at a
slot of `Ω`: the slot after which it was declared. For a slot `x` of `Ω`, write
`E_{<x}` for those positioned strictly before `x`.

Positions are inert for everything in this section. They exist for T2, where
the well-formedness of the entry at `x` is checked against `E_{<x}` and not
against `E`. That single restriction is the whole of "equations interleave with
declarations".

**3.5 Definition (the generated congruence).** `~_E` is the least family of
relations on `Expr Ξ`, for all `Ξ` simultaneously, which

1. is an equivalence relation at each `Ξ`;
2. relates `e` and `e'` for every declaration of `E`;
3. is a **congruence**: if `x` is a head and `args`, `args'` are argumentwise
   related — each pair in its own binding context `Ξ ⋈ Λ` — then
   `ap x args ~_E ap x args'`;
4. is stable under **two-sided substitution**: related expressions substituted
   by pointwise related substitutions remain related.

Clause 3 is congruence for *every* head at once, obtained from the shape of
`Expr` rather than declared. A theory author writes no congruence rule for any
symbol. Because sorts are heads too, clause 3 gives congruence of sort
expressions in their arguments — `A ~ A'` implies `Tm A ~ Tm A'` — with no
further provision.

**3.6 Definition (the quotient monad).** Set
`T_E Ω Λ := Expr (Ω ⋈ Λ) / ~_E`. Clauses 3.5.3–4 are exactly what is needed for
the unit and Kleisli extension of `T` to descend, so `T_E` is again a relative
monad over `J`. Write `𝕊_E := Kl(T_E)`, with an identity-on-objects quotient
functor `𝕊 ⟶ 𝕊_E`.

**3.7 Definition (boundaries and decorations modulo `E`).** Repeat 1.2 and 2.2
in `𝕊_E`: `Bd_E(Ω) := Option (Expr Ω / ~_E)`, and a decoration modulo `E`
assigns `Bd_E`-valued boundaries. Propositions 2.5 and 2.6 hold verbatim.
*Two decorated telescopes differing only by derivably equal boundaries are
equal* — including two differing only by derivably equal sort expressions, which
is what conversion will need.

**3.8 Remark (the two universal properties).** Read in the algebraic direction,
a declaration extends the presented model, and the two kinds of entry are the
two elementary constructions:

| entry | effect on `Ω` | universal property |
|---|---|---|
| `d : [Θ] S` or `d : [Θ] sort` | `Ω ↦ Ω ⋈ d̂` | free extension by a generator of arity `Θ` |
| `q : [Θ] (e ≡ e' : S)` | `E ↦ E ∪ {(e,e')}` | coequalizer of `e, e' : ∗̂ ⇉ Ω ⋈ \|Θ\|` |

A theory *is* a presentation by generators and relations, and
`Expr Ω ↠ Expr Ω / ~_E` is the free cover of the model it presents. Sort entries
and object entries are the same construction; only their boundaries differ.

## 4. Contexts

**4.1 Definition (context).** A *context* is a triple

```
Γ  =  ( |Γ| ,  D ,  E )
```

where `|Γ| ∈ 𝔸`, `D` is a decoration of `|Γ|` over the base `1`, and `E` is a
positioned equational presentation over `|Γ|`. The **erasure** of `Γ` is `|Γ|`.

There is one notion of context, and it holds sorts, operations and equations
alike. Read as syntax it supplies the declarations available; read as a
presentation it supplies generators and relations.

**4.2 Proposition (the raw layer survives erasure).** `RawExpr_Γ := Expr |Γ|`,
on the nose. Equation declarations contribute no slot to `|Γ|`, so erasure is
literally forgetting `D` and `E`.

**4.3 Remark (the stratification).** The definitions are consumed in strict
order, each layer mentioning only the previous ones:

```
Expr |Γ|      plain inductive; mentions no boundary and no equality
~_E           a relation on Expr |Γ|; mentions no well-formedness
Bd_E, QDTel   built from ~_E by 3.7
T2            a predicate on Expr |Γ| indexed by Bd_E-boundaries
```

The last layer may use `~_E` freely, including in side conditions comparing
expressions larger than the one being formed, because `~_E` is already defined
on all of `Expr`. This is why the definitions are not mutually recursive, and it
is what the raw layer buys. Removing `C.Ty` strengthens this: in the earlier
draft the sort signature and the decoration were mutually recursive on rank, and
that recursion is now gone.

**4.4 Remark (morphisms).** `𝕊(|Γ|, |Δ|)` is the set of *raw* interpretations of
`Γ` in `Δ`. The subset preserving `E_Γ` — sending each declared parallel pair to
a `~_{E_Δ}`-equality — is what T2 admits, and it is a proper subset: from
`(x, y, q : x ≡ y)` to `(u, v)` the raw assignment `x ↦ u, y ↦ v` exists but does
not preserve the equation. So the category of contexts is a non-full subcategory
of `𝕊_E`, and in particular is not the Kleisli category of any relative monad on
the slot functor `J`, which sees only free objects.

## 5. Correspondence with the repository, and the cost

| definition | live name | status |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 carrier | `Carrier` | **drop the `Ty` field and the `Ty` argument of `slotAt`** |
| 0.2 raw syntax | `Expr` | drop the class index |
| 0.3 `𝕊` | `Subst`, `SyntaxMonad` | drop the class index; `ArityTyFunc` becomes arity-indexed |
| 1.2 boundary | `ClassifierAt bd Ω τ` | becomes `Option (Expr Ω)` — the `Option` moves from being per-class to per-slot |
| 2.1 precedence | `Precedence` | drop the class index |
| 2.2 decoration | `Decoration`, `DecorationPath` | drop the class index; change the codomain |
| 2.5 module | `DTel` | survives |
| 2.6 monoid | `DTelMon` | survives |
| 3.3–3.4 presentation | `EquationPresentation` | drop the class index; **add positions** |
| 3.5 congruence | `DerivEq`, proved least | survives |
| 3.6 quotient monad | `QExpr`, `quotientMonad` | survives |
| 3.7 mod `E` | `QDecoration`, `QDTel`, `QDTelMon` | survives |
| 4.1 context | — | new, assembled from the above |

**The cost is honest and should be stated.** The class index `τ : C.Ty` appears
in essentially every signature in the 7000 lines of the development. Removing it
is mechanical — every proof gets one fewer index and no proof gets harder — but
it is not small, and it touches `Carrier`, `Renaming`, `Expr`, `Subst`,
`Instantiation`, `MonadLaws`, `Interchange`, `SyntaxMonad`, the whole of
`Typing/` and `Equations/`, and every example.

What is bought: the framework stops presupposing its own judgment forms. Sorts,
operations and equations become three readings of one sequential context, which
is what the design was reaching for, and the sort/decoration mutual recursion
disappears.

**Recoverable if wanted.** A coarse class of an expression is derivable rather
than primitive — the class of `ap x args` is the head of `bnd(x)`. If a
class-sorted raw grammar is needed for an adequacy statement, it can be defined
as a predicate on `Expr` rather than baked into the carrier.

## 6. Open

- Whether the unsorted framework should be recovered as "no sort entries, all
  boundaries inert" or as "one sort entry `⋆` with empty arity". The second is
  more uniform; the first keeps `Expr` free of a spurious head.
- Whether `Bd(Ω) = Option (Expr Ω)` should instead be `Expr Ω` with a
  distinguished sort entry `sort` declared first, making *every* entry an object
  of some sort and removing the `Option`. This is the Tarski-vs-Russell choice
  one level up, and it interacts with universes; worth deciding before 1.2 is
  implemented.
- Whether the erasure `|Γ|` should retain the *positions* of equation
  declarations. It need not for §§0–4, but T2's `E_{<x}` needs them, so they
  must survive somewhere.
