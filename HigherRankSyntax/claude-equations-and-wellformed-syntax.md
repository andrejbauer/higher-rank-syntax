# Equations

This note extends the framework of `boundaries-and-decorations.md` — below, *the
definitions note* — with equations, and says what the syntax of a theory with
equations is. Its numbering (`1.1`, `6.1`, `10.7`, …) is referred to throughout;
nothing else is presupposed.

---

## 0. What has to be added, and why

The definitions note ends at 12.1 with an unfinished obligation. In the theory
of dependent sums (§8) the boundary of `snd` is `tm (B (fst A B p))`, so the
boundary of `snd` applied to `pair A B a b` is

```
tm (B (fst A B (pair A B a b)))
```

while the type wanted for it is `tm (B a)`. These are distinct elements of
`Expr`, and must be, since 2.1 generates `Expr` freely. They become equal only
once the theory's equation

```
fst A B (pair A B a b) = a
```

is imposed and `Expr` is replaced by a quotient. So a theory must be able to
declare equations, and the object of interest is not `Expr` but a quotient of
part of it. Four things have to be settled:

1. what an equation declaration is (§1);
2. what relation on expressions a set of them generates (§2–§5);
3. which expressions and declarations are well formed (§5–§6);
4. what structure the well-formed expressions modulo that relation carry (§9).

The short answer, and the reason the note is organized as it is, is that (2)
cannot be settled before (3): the relation generated by the equations of a
theory cannot be defined over raw syntax alone, and the attempt to do so is
unsound. §§2–3 show this on two five-line theories; §§4–9 give the design that
results.

---

## 1. Equations, and presentations

**Definition 1.1 (equation).** An **equation over `Ω`** is a boundary together
with two expressions that are to be identified as things of that boundary:

```
Equation Ω  :=  (boundary : Bd(Ω)) × (left right : Expr Ω).
```

By 5.1 the boundary is either `sort` or `of S`, and these are the two equality
judgements of a generalized algebraic theory: `left ≡ right sort`, and
`left ≡ right : S`.

**Definition 1.2 (axiom).** An **axiom over `Ω`** is a decorated telescope `Θ`
over `Ω` — its **metavariables** — together with an `Equation (Ω ⋈ |Θ|)`, where
`|Θ|` is the underlying arity of `Θ`. It is written

```
[ z₁ : β₁, …, zₙ : βₙ ]   l ≡ r : S
```

in the notation of 7.1, with the shared boundary after the colon.

An axiom is a declaration (6.2, 7.1) minus the slot: the same telescope, the
same boundary written over it, but contributing nothing to any arity.

**Definition 1.3 (presentation).** A **presentation over `Ω`** is a sequence of
declarations and axioms, each written over `Ω` extended by the arities declared
before it. As an inductive family, indexed by the arity declared so far:

```
nil                                                       : Presentation Ω 1

declare (P : Presentation Ω Γ)
        (Δ : DecoratedTelescope (Ω ⋈ Γ))                  : Presentation Ω (Γ ⋈ |Δ|)

assume  (P : Presentation Ω Γ)
        (a : Axiom (Ω ⋈ Γ))                               : Presentation Ω Γ
```

`declare` extends the index by the declared arity; `assume` leaves it alone,
which is Definition 1.2 made structural. Two ordinary recursions on this family
give

```
P.decoration : Decoration Ω Γ        empty (10.8) at nil, concatenated (10.7) at
                                     declare, unchanged at assume
P.axioms     : a family of axioms    empty at nil, weakened at declare, extended
               over Ω ⋈ Γ            at assume
```

so a presentation erases to a decorated telescope with the same arity, together
with a list of axioms. A **theory** is a presentation over `1`.

**Example 1.4.** The theory of dependent sums is §8's six declarations followed
by two axioms:

```
beta_fst : [ A : ty, B : [x : tm A] ty, a : tm A, b : tm (B a) ]
             fst A B (pair A B a b)  ≡  a  :  tm A

beta_snd : [ A : ty, B : [x : tm A] ty, a : tm A, b : tm (B a) ]
             snd A B (pair A B a b)  ≡  b  :  tm (B a)
```

Each metavariable telescope is a decorated telescope over the six declared
symbols, and is the same one that `pair` has as its arity.

**Definition 1.5 (what an axiom asserts).** An axiom is a statement quantified
over its metavariables: *for all `z₁ : β₁, …, zₙ : βₙ`, `l` and `r` are the same
thing of boundary `S`.* Only rank-0 telescopes and closed expressions appear in
§§2–3, and for those the reading is elementary. For a theory each of whose
entries is a sort with no arguments, an object with no arguments, or an object
with one argument, an **interpretation** assigns

- a set `⟦s⟧` to each sort entry `s`;
- an element `⟦c⟧ ∈ ⟦S⟧` to each object entry `c : [ ] of S`;
- a function `⟦g⟧ : ⟦S⟧ → ⟦S'⟧` to each object entry `g : [z : of S] of S'`;

and it **satisfies** an axiom `[z₁ : of S₁, …] l ≡ r : S` when `⟦l⟧ = ⟦r⟧` for
every assignment of elements of `⟦S₁⟧, …` to `z₁, …`. This is all the semantics
§§2–3 need; the general notion is not defined in this note.

## 2. The design to be rejected

Here is the design that suggests itself, and that the earlier drafts in this
repository adopt. It is worth stating in full, because it is attractive, and
because what defeats it is what shapes the rest of the note.

**Definition 2.1 (the congruence over raw syntax).** For a set `E` of axioms,
`~` is the least family of relations on `Expr Ξ`, for every arity `Ξ` at once,
which

1. is an equivalence relation at each `Ξ`;
2. relates `l` and `r` for each axiom of `E`;
3. is a congruence: if `args` and `args'` are related argumentwise, each pair in
   its own extended arity, then `ap x args ~ ap x args'`;
4. is closed under substitution: if `e ~ e'` and `σ(z) ~ θ(z)` for every slot
   `z`, then `e[σ] ~ e'[θ]`.

Clause 4 is what turns an axiom into a schema: substituting for the
metavariables produces its instances.

**2.2 Why this is the design one wants.** Every definition in it is over raw
syntax, so the layers are strictly ordered and nothing is mutually recursive:

```
Expr Ξ        2.1 of the definitions note; mentions no boundary
~             a relation on Expr; mentions no well-formedness
Bd, DTel      5.1 and 6.1 repeated over Expr/~
judgements    predicates on Expr, indexed by boundaries modulo ~
```

Three things then come for free. Substitution and the module and monoid
structure of 10.3–10.9 descend to the quotient, so the whole tower is had a
second time by instantiation. Conversion is not a rule: two boundaries related
by `~` are literally the same element of `Bd(Expr/~)`, so a judgement indexed by
one is the same proposition as the judgement indexed by the other. And 12.1's
obligation is discharged before any typing exists at all, since
`tm (B (fst A B (pair A B a b))) ~ tm (B a)` follows from `beta_fst` by clause 3
alone.

**2.3 It is unsound.** Take the theory

```
A  : [ ]            sort
B  : [ ]            sort
a₁ : [ ]            of A
a₂ : [ ]            of A
b  : [ ]            of B
```

with one axiom

```
q  : [ y : of B ]   y  ≡  b  :  B
```

— *every thing of sort `B` is `b`*. Clause 4 lets the metavariable `y` be
replaced by **any** expression, in particular by `a₁` and by `a₂`:

```
a₁ ~ b        a₂ ~ b        hence   a₁ ~ a₂ .
```

The interpretation `⟦A⟧ = {0,1}`, `⟦B⟧ = {∗}`, `⟦a₁⟧ = 0`, `⟦a₂⟧ = 1`,
`⟦b⟧ = ∗` satisfies `q` in the sense of 1.5, and `⟦a₁⟧ ≠ ⟦a₂⟧`. So `~`
identifies expressions that the theory does not identify.

**2.4 What went wrong.** `q` asserts something about the things of sort `B`.
Clause 4 instantiates it at `a₁`, which is not one: it instantiates a universally
quantified statement outside the domain it is quantified over. The domain is
recorded — the metavariable `y` is declared with boundary `of B` in the axiom's
telescope — and clause 4 ignores it.

No dependency, no binding and no higher rank is involved, so this is not a
defect that appears only in exotic theories.

## 3. Restricting the instances needs the typing judgement

The repair is forced: clause 4 may instantiate an axiom only at fillers that
*are* things of the declared boundary. The question is what that means, and the
answer decides the architecture, because "`e` is a thing of boundary `β`" is
precisely the judgement that the typing layer is supposed to define. Making it a
premise of `~` makes `~` and the typing judgement mutually recursive, and 2.2's
strict layering is lost.

So it is worth trying the cheapest surrogate first.

**3.1 The surrogate.** In this framework every head carries a declared boundary:
if `x : Ω ∋ α` is a slot and `D` the decoration, `D` gives `x` a boundary over
`Ω ⋈ before x ⋈ α` by 6.1, and instantiating `x`'s own arguments into it yields
a boundary of `ap x args` over `Ω` — a total function of raw syntax, computed
from the head alone (Definition 4.1 below). Require of a filler only that this
computed boundary be `~` its metavariable's declared boundary. The condition
mentions `~` and no typing judgement, so 2.2's layering survives.

**3.2 It is unsound too.** Take the theory

```
A  : [ ]            sort
S  : [ ]            sort
c₁ : [ ]            of S
c₂ : [ ]            of S
g  : [ z : of A ]   of A
```

with one axiom

```
q  : [ w : of A ]   c₁  ≡  c₂  :  S
```

— *if there is anything of sort `A`, then `c₁ = c₂`*. Note that the two sides do
not mention `w`: the axiom is conditional on `A` being inhabited.

`A` is not inhabited. The only entry whose boundary is `of A` is `g`, and `g`
needs a thing of sort `A` to produce one, so there is no well-formed closed
expression of boundary `of A` — expressions are finite. Accordingly the
interpretation `⟦A⟧ = ∅`, `⟦S⟧ = {0,1}`, `⟦c₁⟧ = 0`, `⟦c₂⟧ = 1`, `⟦g⟧` the
empty function satisfies `q` vacuously and separates `c₁` and `c₂`.

But `g c₁` is a raw expression, and its computed boundary is `of A` — read off
from `g`'s declaration, without looking at the argument. The surrogate therefore
accepts `σ(w) := g c₁` and derives `c₁ ~ c₂`.

**3.3 Conclusion.** The filler condition has to be hereditary: `g c₁` fails to be
a thing of sort `A` because its *argument* is not a thing of sort `A`, and only a
condition that descends into arguments can see this. That condition is
well-formedness. Hence

> **the relation generated by the equations of a theory and the well-formedness
> judgement are mutually inductive.**

**3.4 What is lost, and what is not.** Lost is the layering of 2.2: `~` is no
longer definable before the judgements, so boundaries cannot be quotiented in
advance, and conversion is no longer literally the identity of quotient elements
(4.3 gives what replaces it).

Not lost is the raw layer itself. `Expr` is still defined first, freely, from the
carrier alone; well-formedness and equality are two `Prop`s over it, given by a
single simultaneous induction. Nothing becomes inductive–recursive, and a premise
may still mention equality at expressions larger than the one being formed, since
an inductive definition requires positivity and not decrease. The quotient, the
module and the monoid of 10.3–10.9 are then taken at the end rather than in the
middle, and §9 is where they reappear.

## 4. The boundary of an expression

The surrogate of 3.1 fails as a soundness condition, but the function it is built
from is what makes the judgements of §5 small, so it is worth having as a
definition.

**Definition 4.1.** Let `D` be a decoration of `Ω` over the base `1`. Every
expression over `Ω` has a **boundary**

```
D.boundaryOf : Expr Ω → Bd(Ω)
```

given by `D.boundaryOf (ap x args) = ` the boundary `D` declares for `x`,
weakened along the inclusion of `before x` into `Ω`, and instantiated by `args`.
With the three operations that already exist:

```lean
def Decoration.boundaryOf {Ω : C.Arity} (D : Decoration 1 Ω) : Expr Ω → Boundary Ω
  | .ap x args =>
      Boundary.instantiate args (Boundary.rename (C.inclusion x ⇑ʳ _) (D.boundary x))
```

This is total and involves no recursion: only the head is inspected. It extends
`D.boundary`, which gives a boundary to a slot, to expressions. It exists because
a decoration declares a boundary for every slot — the content of Cartmell's
requirement that the type of a term is always an instance of a declared sort
symbol, in the form 6.1 gives it.

**4.2 How it moves.** Two different statements, and the difference matters in §9.

- *Along a renaming, strictly.* If `ρ` is a renaming under which the target's
  decoration restricts to the source's — the weakening `Ω →ʳ Ω ⋈ |Δ|` for a
  telescope `Δ`, by `concatenate_here_inl` — then
  `boundaryOf (⟦ρ⟧ e) = Boundary.rename ρ (boundaryOf e)`.
- *Along a substitution, up to `≈`.* For a well-formed substitution `σ` in the
  sense of 5.3, `boundaryOf (e[σ]) ≈ (boundaryOf e)[σ]`. Equality cannot be
  expected: the head `x` of `e` is replaced by `σ x`, whose declared boundary is
  only `≈` the transport of `x`'s. After the quotient of §9 the `≈` becomes an
  equality and this is naturality on the nose.

**4.3 What it buys.**

- *One judgement instead of two.* "`e` is a thing of boundary `β`" need not be an
  inductive judgement of its own: it is `Wf Ψ e ∧ boundaryOf e ≈ β`, for the
  predicate of 5.1 and the relation of 5.2. There is no separate checking
  judgement to define, and no rules relating the two.
- *Conversion is transitivity.* If `β ≈ β'` then a thing of boundary `β` is a
  thing of boundary `β'`, by transitivity of `≈` alone. It is not a rule and not
  an admissibility theorem. This is what 2.2's quotient boundaries were wanted
  for, and it survives 3.3 unchanged.
- The coarse class of an expression — the head of its boundary — is derived
  rather than primitive, so a class-sorted grammar can be recovered as a
  predicate if one is ever wanted for an adequacy statement.

## 5. Well-formedness and equality

Fix a presentation `P` and a decorated telescope `Γ` over `|P|`. Write

```
Ψ  :=  P.telescope ⋈ Γ
```

for the **ambient**: a decorated telescope over the base `1`, by 10.7, whose
underlying arity is `|P| ⋈ |Γ|`. Both relations below are stated for `Ψ` and are
defined by one simultaneous induction. Neither asserts anything about `P` or
about `Γ`; §6 is what asserts.

**Definition 5.1 (well-formedness).** `Wf Ψ e` for `e ∈ Expr |Ψ|`, by the single
clause: for `x : |Ψ| ∋ α` and `args : Subst α |Ψ|`,

```
   for every  z : α ∋ Λ    Wf (Ψ ⋈ Δ_z) (args z)
   for every  z : α ∋ Λ    boundaryOf (args z)  ≈  β_z
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                     Wf Ψ (ap x args)
```

where, writing `D` for the decoration of `Ψ`:

- `β_z` is the boundary `D` assigns to `z` inside `x` — an element of
  `Bd(|Ψ| ⋈ before x ⋈ before z ⋈ Λ)` by 6.1 — weakened along the inclusion of
  `before x`, and with the arguments preceding `z`, namely `args ∘ C.inclusion z`,
  substituted for `before z`; this is `Boundary.act` at depth `Λ`;
- `Δ_z` is the decorated telescope whose arity is `Λ` and whose decoration is the
  one `D` gives inside `z`, transported the same way; this is
  `Decoration.substitute`.

The two premises are the halves of *each argument sits at the boundary its
declaration gives it, instantiated by its earlier siblings*. The recursive
premise is stated at `Ψ ⋈ Δ_z`, so **Definition 5.1 consumes the concatenation of
10.7**: the monoid of decorated telescopes is what a well-formedness judgement is
written against.

**Definition 5.2 (equality).** `e ≈ e'` for `e, e' ∈ Expr |Ψ|` is generated by

1. equivalence: reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity;
2. congruence: `ap x args ≈ ap x args'` when `args z ≈ args' z` for every `z`,
   each in its own extended ambient `Ψ ⋈ Δ_z`;
3. axioms at well-formed instances: for an axiom `(Θ, β, l, r)` of `P`, weakened
   into `|Ψ|`, and a substitution `σ : |Θ| ⇒ |Ψ|` which is **well formed** — each
   `σ z` satisfies `Wf Ψ (σ z)` and `boundaryOf (σ z) ≈` the boundary `Θ` gives
   `z`, instantiated by the earlier fillers —

   ```
   l[σ]  ≈  r[σ] ;
   ```

4. substitution: if `e ≈ e'` and `σ, θ` are both well formed as in 3 with
   `σ z ≈ θ z` for every `z`, then `e[σ] ≈ e'[θ]`.

Clause 3 is 2.1(2,4) with the domain restriction of 3.3 imposed. Clause 2 is
congruence at every head at once, so a theory declares no congruence rule for any
symbol; since sorts are heads too, it gives `A ≈ A' ⟹ tm A ≈ tm A'` with nothing
further provided.

`≈` is extended from expressions to `Bd(|Ψ|)` by the case split of 5.1 of the
definitions note: `sort ≈ sort`; `of S ≈ of S'` when `S ≈ S'`; `sort` and `of S`
are never related. That extension is the relation meant in 4.3, in the second
premise of 5.1, in clause 3 above, and in §6; it is an equivalence relation
because clause 1 makes `≈` one on expressions.

**5.3 What must be proved.** Everything below is a theorem about the definitions,
not part of them. `Wf P` is the hypothesis of §6.

| statement | note |
|---|---|
| `Wf` is preserved by well-formed substitution | the load-bearing one; (L4) of the classical substitution lemma |
| well-formed substitutions contain the identity and are closed under composition | makes contexts a category, §9 |
| `Wf Ψ (η x)`, and `boundaryOf (η x)` is `x`'s declared boundary | the variable rule |
| `boundaryOf` moves as in 4.2 | strictly for renamings, up to `≈` for substitutions |
| presuppositions: given `Wf P`, if `e ≈ e'` then `Wf Ψ e ↔ Wf Ψ e'`, and then `boundaryOf e ≈ boundaryOf e'` | 5.5 |
| `Wf` and `≈` are invariant under replacing `Ψ`'s decoration by an `≈`-equal one | needed for the index of §9 |

The first, the second and the presuppositions are one simultaneous induction, and
they are the bulk of the work. The hypothesis `Wf P` is not decoration: for an
unchecked presentation, clause 5.2(3) can relate a well-formed expression to
junk, which is 2.3 again.

**5.4 Clause 4 is a rule, not a theorem.** It could be dropped and proved
instead, giving a smaller relation. Two reasons not to:

- The substitution lemma needs it. Substituting into `Wf Ψ (ap x args)` carries
  the premise `boundaryOf (args z) ≈ β_z` to `boundaryOf (args z)[σ] ≈ β_z[σ]`,
  which *is* clause 4. As a rule, the substitution lemma is an induction; as a
  theorem, it and clause 4 must be proved by one simultaneous induction.
- The presuppositions need it in a sharper form: deriving `Wf Ψ (ap x args')`
  from `Wf Ψ (ap x args)` and `args ≈ args'` requires `β_z[args] ≈ β_z[args']`,
  which is clause 4 with the same expression on both sides. (That case is
  delicate anyway: `β_z` depends on the arguments preceding `z`, so the argument
  runs over the slots of `α` in precedence order.)

Nothing is lost semantically, since every instance of the rule has well-formed
fillers. What is lost is minimality of `≈`, recoverable later as a `least`-style
theorem: the relation without clause 4 is already closed under well-formed
substitution, hence equal to the one with it.

**5.5 `≈` is unindexed, and its presuppositions are theorems.** The alternative
is the finitary-type-theory shape, a judgement `Ψ ⊢ e ≡ e' ⇐ β` carrying the
boundary, in which *both sides are well formed at a common boundary* holds by
construction and moving between `≈`-equal boundaries needs a conversion rule.
Here the boundary is computable (4.1), so carrying it as an index would duplicate
information and force a coherence between the index and `boundaryOf`; instead the
invariant is the presupposition theorem of 5.3. The retreat to the indexed form,
should that theorem prove worse than expected, changes the signature of `≈` and
nothing above it.

**5.6 The inert case.** If every declared boundary in `P` is `sort`, then every
computed boundary is `sort`, every premise of 5.1 holds, `Wf` is total, and
5.2(3)'s restriction is vacuous: `≈` is exactly 2.1's `~`. So the unsorted and
simply-sorted theories — monoids, the λ-calculus — are the degenerate instance of
these definitions, and 2.1 is not wasted. It is only for theories whose
boundaries constrain that 2.1 and 5.2 differ.

## 6. When a presentation is well formed

Definitions 5.1 and 5.2 are stated for an arbitrary presentation: they take its
axioms as given and assert nothing about them. Nothing requires the two sides of
an axiom to have its stated boundary, and nothing should — "has boundary `S`" is
a judgement of §5, so requiring it in Definition 1.2 would index `Axiom` by a
derivation and re-create the mutual dependency that the raw layer exists to
break. The requirement belongs here instead.

**Definition 6.1.** Say a telescope `Δ` over `Ψ` is well formed when every slot's
binding arity is a well-formed telescope over the ambient extended by what
precedes it, and every boundary is either `sort` or `of S` with `Wf S` and
`boundaryOf S = sort` — an ordinary recursion on `Δ`, using 5.1 but not mutual
with it. Then `Wf P` is an inductive predicate on the family of 1.3:

```
Wf nil

Wf P     Δ is a well-formed telescope, against P.axioms
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Wf (declare P Δ)

Wf P     a.Θ is a well-formed telescope, and a.left and a.right are well formed
         with boundaryOf ≈ a.boundary, against P.axioms
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Wf (assume P a)
```

**6.2 This is not circular.** For *every* presentation `P`, well formed or not,
5.1 and 5.2 define `Wf` and `≈`. Definition 6.1 is a separate induction, on the
structure of `P`, whose premises mention the judgements of the sub-presentation
`P'` — never the axioms the step itself contributes. So the definitions are
consumed in the order

```
4.1  boundaryOf                       from the decoration alone
5.1  Wf   ⎫ mutually, for an arbitrary presentation and an arbitrary ambient
5.2  ≈    ⎭
6.1  well-formed telescope            a recursion using 5.1
6.1  Wf P                             an induction on P using the above at P'
```

**6.3 Each step is checked in a quotient certified by the steps before it, and
that is load-bearing.** By induction on 6.1, every premise is stated against
axioms whose own well-formedness has already been established. In particular an
axiom cannot be used to justify itself: at the moment `assume P a` is checked,
`a` is not among `P.axioms`. Had the axioms instead been collected into one set
over the final arity, it would have been.

The same order-dependence appears positively. In the theory of 1.4 the boundary
of `snd A B (pair A B a b)` computes to `tm (B (fst A B (pair A B a b)))`, and
6.1 requires it to be `≈` the stated boundary `tm (B a)` of `beta_snd`. It is, by
5.2(3) at `beta_fst` followed by 5.2(2) at the heads `B` and `tm`. Declare
`beta_snd` before `beta_fst` and the check fails. This is the concrete form of
12.1's obligation, and it is discharged here rather than in §2.

## 7. What is not admitted

**7.1 Axioms in a context.** 1.3 puts axioms in a presentation, and they are not
admitted anywhere else — in particular not in the telescope a judgement is made
in, nor in a symbol's binding arity. The reason is §9's structure and is worth
stating here.

A substitution `Γ ⇒ Δ` is determined slotwise: 10.1 gives an expression for each
slot of `Γ`. An axiom of `Γ` contributes no slot, so nothing in the data of a
substitution refers to it, and the requirement that a substitution *respect* the
axiom — that `l[σ] ≈ r[σ]` — is an extra condition, not part of the slotwise
data. It cannot be pushed into the slot functor either: the condition mentions
the very `σ` being given.

Concretely, from `(x : A, y : A, q : x ≡ y : A)` to `(u : A, v : A)` the
assignment `x ↦ u, y ↦ v` is a perfectly good substitution and does not respect
`q`. So contexts carrying axioms form a category whose morphisms are a proper
subcollection of the substitutions, and by 10.2 that is not the Kleisli category
of any relative monad over the slot functor. Algebraically this is the expected
statement: a presentation with relations presents a model that is not free, and
Kleisli categories see only free ones. The theory itself may carry axioms because
it is a fixed prefix that substitution never touches.

The proper target for hypothetical equations is therefore the category of models
*presented* by such contexts, in which that subcollection is full. That is a
different theorem from §9 and a separate development.

**7.2 Equational premises.** An argument position occupied by an equation,

```
cancel : [ x y z : tm M, (x·z ≡ y·z : tm M) ]   x ≡ y : tm M
```

— the essentially-algebraic and partial-Horn world — is likewise not admitted.
The cost is a translation, not a loss of theories.

*Most examples do not need them.* Composition in a category is the classic
operation with an equational premise `dom f ≡ cod g`; the generalized algebraic
theory of categories has no premise, because `hom : [x : obj, y : obj] sort` puts
the condition in the index. That translation — a condition between *indices*
becomes a dependent sort — is Cartmell's, and it covers pullbacks and partial
operations on typed data generally.

*What remains is expressible already.* A genuine Horn clause, an equation
conditional on another equation, is obtained by internalizing the equality
judgement:

```
Id      : [ A : ty, u : tm A, v : tm A ]              sort
refl    : [ A : ty, u : tm A ]                        Id A u u
reflect : [ A, u, v, p : Id A u v ]                   u ≡ v : tm A
irrel   : [ A, u, v, p : Id A u v, q : Id A u v ]     p ≡ q : Id A u v
```

`reflect` and `irrel` are axioms in the sense of 1.2 — their premises are
ordinary slots, not equations, so §5 is untouched. `Id A u v` is then inhabited
exactly when `u ≈ v`: `refl A u` has boundary `Id A u u`, which is `≈ Id A u v`
whenever `u ≈ v`, so by 4.3 it is a thing of that boundary; conversely
`reflect`. `irrel` makes the sort a subsingleton, so a model of the extended
theory carries no data beyond a model of the original. Cancellativity becomes

```
cancel : [ x y z : tm M, p : Id M (x·z) (y·z) ]   x ≡ y : tm M
```

with the same instances, since 5.2(3) demands a well-formed `p` and those exist
exactly for the derivable equalities.

*What admitting them would cost.* 5.1 would need a third premise, that the
arguments *satisfy* the equations in the head's binding arity; and 1.3 would
become recursive — the binding arity of a declaration would carry a presentation
rather than a decorated telescope — which changes `DecoratedTelescope`, the
module of 10.3, the tensor of 10.6 and the monoid of 10.9. This is the one
deferral worth revisiting if a theory turns up whose `Id`-encoding is
unacceptable.

## 8. Higher-rank sorts

Nothing in §§1–7 restricts the rank (1.3) of a sort entry, and the examples so
far do not exercise this: `ty` is rank 0 and `tm` is rank 1, which is all
ordinary type theory needs — Cartmell's observation that binding can be confined
to the *operations*. A sort of rank ≥ 2 is a judgement form one of whose indices
binds. Two are worth having as examples, because they are the theories whose
declarations the machinery of §§4–6 has not yet been run against.

**8.1 Rank 2: the section judgement.** Declared after `ty` and `tm` of §8:

```
sect : [ A : ty, B : [x : tm A] ty ]         sort        entry ⟨nil, un⟩, rank 2
ev   : [ A, B, s : sect A B, a : tm A ]      tm (B a)
mk   : [ A, B, b : [x : tm A] tm (B x) ]     sect A B
```

with

```
sect_beta : [ A, B, b : [x : tm A] tm (B x), a : tm A ]   ev A B (mk A B b) a ≡ b a : tm (B a)
sect_eta  : [ A, B, s : sect A B ]                        mk A B ([x] ev A B s x) ≡ s : sect A B
```

`sect A B` is the hypothetical judgement `x : tm A ⊢ b(x) : tm (B x)`, reified as
a sort. Its entry is `Σ`'s from §8, used as a sort rather than as an operation,
so it needs no machinery that §8 does not already need.

What it buys is the natural-model presentation of `Π`. Instead of an
introduction rule whose *argument binds*,

```
lam : [ A, B, b : [x : tm A] tm (B x) ]   tm (Π A B)
```

one declares an isomorphism of two sorts, whose arguments bind nothing:

```
Π    : [ A, B ]                  ty
lam  : [ A, B, s : sect A B ]    tm (Π A B)
app  : [ A, B, f : tm (Π A B) ]  sect A B

pi_beta : [ A, B, s : sect A B ]        app A B (lam A B s) ≡ s : sect A B
pi_eta  : [ A, B, f : tm (Π A B) ]      lam A B (app A B f) ≡ f : tm (Π A B)
```

Binding then survives only in the *indices* `B`, never in the arguments of a term
former — which is what `Tm(Π A B) ≅ Sect(A, B)` says in a natural model, and what
a judgement form closed under Π-along-representables is in Uemura's setting.
**Rank-2 sorts are hypothetical judgements**; a second-order theory becomes a
first-order one by giving its second-order metavariables a sort to inhabit.

**8.2 Rank 3: a rule as an inhabitant.** An index that binds something which
itself binds is a *schema*. In a fragment of first-order arithmetic:

```
tm   : [ ]                                     sort
form : [ ]                                     sort
prf  : [ φ : form ]                            sort
0    : [ ]                                     tm
s    : [ n : tm ]                              tm
⇒    : [ φ : form, ψ : form ]                  form
∀    : [ φ : [x : tm] form ]                   form        rank 2 operation
sch  : [ Φ : [ P : [x : tm] form ] form ]      sort        entry ⟨⟨un⟩⟩, rank 3
inst : [ Φ, r : sch Φ, P : [x : tm] form ]     prf (Φ P)
```

`sch Φ` is the judgement *`Φ(P)` is derivable uniformly in the unary predicate
`P`*, and `inst` instantiates it. The induction rule of arithmetic is then a
single inhabitant

```
ind : [ ]   sch ([P] P 0 ⇒ (∀ [x] (P x ⇒ P (s x))) ⇒ ∀ [x] P x)
```

rather than an axiom schema. **Rank-2 sorts internalize open terms; rank-3 sorts
internalize rules.** Each meta-level a theory talks about costs one rank on the
sort, which is the same accounting as the rank of an operation.

**8.3 What these test.** They are the right stress cases for §§4–6, and for
different reasons.

| feature | first exercised by |
|---|---|
| a boundary whose head is a sort applied to a *binding* argument (`of (sect A B)`) | 8.1 |
| 5.1 at a head that is a sort of rank 2 — the nested decoration of a *sort's* arity | 8.1 |
| an axiom whose metavariable telescope has a rank-1 entry (`b`) | 8.1, `sect_beta` |
| an axiom whose two sides are of a rank-2 *sort* (`sect_eta`) | 8.1 |
| 5.2(3) at a rank-1 filler — instantiating a metavariable by a schema | 8.2, `inst` |
| an axiom whose metavariable telescope has a rank-2 entry | 8.2 |
| `Expr.η` at rank 2 — writing `sch Φ` at all | 8.2 |

## 9. The categorical picture

Nothing in this section is proved in this note; it says what the objects of §§5–6
assemble into, and which of the definitions note's structures are reused.

**9.1 The raw picture, recalled.** By 10.1–10.9 there is a category `𝕊` of
arities and substitutions, which is the Kleisli category of the relative monad

```
J Ω = (α ↦ Ω ∋ α)          T Ω = (α ↦ Expr (Ω ⋈ α))
```

over the slot functor; the functors `𝕊 → Set` are its modules; `DTel` is one,
carrying a shape to arities (10.5); arity-shaped modules have a tensor (10.6);
and `DTel` is a monoid object for it (10.9), with multiplication the dependent
concatenation of 10.7.

**9.2 The direction of `𝕊`.** A substitution `σ : Γ ⇒ Ω` assigns an expression
over `Ω` to each slot of `Γ`, so in the notation of type theory it is a context
morphism `Ω → Γ`. **`𝕊` is the opposite of the category of contexts**, and a
module `𝕊 → Set` is a presheaf on contexts. This is why `DTel` is covariant.

**9.3 The typed objects.** Fix a well-formed theory `P` with arity `Ω`.

```
Ctx        well-formed decorated telescopes over Ω                     (6.1)
Sub Γ Ξ    { σ : Subst |Γ| (Ω ⋈ |Ξ|)  //  σ is well formed }           (5.2(3))
Tel Γ      well-formed decorated telescopes over Ω ⋈ |Γ|, modulo ≈     (6.1, 5.3)
Tm Γ Δ     { e : Expr (Ω ⋈ |Γ| ⋈ |Δ|)  //  Wf (P ⋈ Γ ⋈ Δ) e } / ≈
```

Two facts make this well posed, and both are in 5.3's list: well-formed
substitutions contain the identity and compose, so `Ctx` is a category; and `Wf`
is invariant under replacing a decoration by an `≈`-equal one, so `Tm Γ Δ`
depends only on the class of `Δ` in `Tel Γ`.

The forgetful map to the raw layer is a **refinement system** in the sense of
Melliès–Zeilberger: an object of `Ctx` is a raw arity with structure, a morphism
of `Ctx` is a raw substitution with a *property*. Every structure map below is
therefore the raw one, restricted and then descended through the quotient — the
monad laws included.

**9.4 Why the index moves.** `Tel` is not a constant: a substitution transports
telescopes, `σ ⋆ : Tel Γ → Tel Ξ`, by the module action of 10.3 followed by 5.3's
substitution lemma. It cannot be replaced by the constant set of arities, because
two slots with the same arity can carry different decorations:

```
Γ = [ A : ty, B : ty, f : [z : tm A] tm A, g : [z : tm B] tm B ]
```

`f` and `g` both have underlying arity `un`, but a well-formed substitution must
send `f` to something well formed *given `z : tm A`* and `g` to something well
formed *given `z : tm B`*. There is no single set of "well-formed expressions of
arity `un` over `Ξ`" for both to land in.

**9.5 The structure.** What §5 and §6 produce is therefore

```
Tel : Ctx ⥤ Type                     a submodule of DTel, and a monoid object
                                     for the tensor of 10.6 — the unit is the
                                     empty telescope, the multiplication is 10.7

Tm  : (Γ : Ctx) → Tel Γ → Type       with
  η    : a slot of Γ with decorated arity Δ  ↦  an element of Tm Γ Δ
  act  : (σ : Sub Γ Ξ) → Tm Γ Δ → Tm Ξ (σ ⋆ Δ)
```

together with the identity and composition laws for `act`, and `act σ (η x) = σ x`
— the relative-monad laws of `SyntaxMonad`, restricted and descended.

This is the same shape as 9.1's monad with one substitution: *the constant index
set of arities is replaced by the module `Tel`*. If one wants it as a
`RelativeMonad` in the sense of `RelativeMonad/Basic.lean`, the base is the total
category of families along that module,

```
objects     (Γ : Ctx, f : Tel Γ → Type)
morphisms   (σ : Sub Γ Ξ,  φ : ∀ Δ, f Δ → g (σ ⋆ Δ))
```

with `ArityFunc` the case of the constant module. That packaging is available but
is not needed for anything above: because `Sub Γ Ξ` is a subtype of a raw Kleisli
hom, the substitutions are given all at once and the action on `Tm` is stated
directly.

**9.6 The boundary map.** By 4.2 and 5.3, `boundaryOf` respects `≈` and commutes
with substitution up to `≈`. Both become strict in the quotient, so

```
boundaryOf : Tm Γ Δ → Bd(Ω ⋈ |Γ| ⋈ |Δ|)/≈
```

is a natural transformation. The boundary is therefore not an index of `Tm` but a
map out of it, and the set of *terms of a given type* is a fibre:

```
Tm Γ Δ β  :=  boundaryOf⁻¹(β).
```

**9.7 What it is, in familiar terms.** Reading 9.2 backwards:

| category with families | here |
|---|---|
| category of contexts | `Ctx`, with `Sub` reversed |
| presheaf of types `Ty(Γ)` | well-formed boundaries modulo `≈`, i.e. the image of 9.6 |
| presheaf of telescopes | `Tel Γ` — the primitive notion here |
| terms `Tm(Γ, A)` | the fibre of 9.6 over `A`, at `Δ = 1` |
| substitution `A[σ]` | the module action `σ ⋆` |
| comprehension `Γ.A` | context extension `Γ ⋈ Δ`, the monoid multiplication of 10.9 |
| weakening `Γ.A → Γ` | the substitution `Γ ⇒ Γ ⋈ Δ` that is the identity on `Γ` |

Two differences from an ordinary category with families are worth naming. First,
`Tel` — not `Ty` — is primitive: comprehension by a *single* type requires the
carrier to have a one-slot arity, which 1.1 does not provide, whereas telescopic
extension is 10.7 and always exists. Second, an entry of a telescope may itself
bind, so a context may declare metavariables and schemas; `Tel Γ` contains the
rank-1 and rank-2 entries of §8 as well as ordinary types.

**9.8 What the equations did.** They quotiented `Tm` and, through 9.6, the types;
they did not touch `Ctx`. Read algebraically, the two formers of 1.3 are the two
elementary constructions on a presented model,

| former | effect | universal property |
|---|---|---|
| `declare Δ` | extends the arity by `\|Δ\|` | free extension by generators of interface `Δ` |
| `assume a` | extends the axioms | coequalizer of the parallel pair `l, r` |

and a theory is a presentation by generators and relations, with
`Expr ↠ Tm` the free cover of the model it presents. Contexts, by 7.1, use only
the first former, which is exactly why `Ctx` is a category of free objects and
9.5 is a monad at all.

## 11. Settled, and open

Settled by this note:

- equations live only in a presentation, never in a context or a binding arity
  (7.1);
- equational premises are not admitted; the translation of 7.2 is the
  replacement;
- 5.2(4) is a rule (5.4);
- `≈` is unindexed and its presuppositions are theorems (5.5).

Open, and deliberately so:

- hypothetical equations, and the category of presented models they call for
  (7.1);
- equational premises, if a theory turns up whose `Id`-encoding is unacceptable
  (7.2);
- the semantics: 1.5 defines interpretations only for the fragment §§2–3 need,
  and the soundness statement that §§5–6 are designed to satisfy — every model
  validates `≈` — is not formulated here;
- whether `Boundary`, `Decoration` and the module and monoid of 10.3–10.9 should
  be made parametric in the monad, so that their quotient versions are
  instantiations rather than copies. Nothing in passes 1–9 needs it, since the
  quotient is taken once and at the end.
