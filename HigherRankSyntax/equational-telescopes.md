# Well-formed equational telescopes: a monoid, and a natural model

This note gives the definitions needed to state and formalize:

> **Theorem.** Over the category of contexts, the well-formed decorated telescopes
> — carrying as their equivalence the equality generated by the equations they
> contain — form a presheaf `𝒯₀` which is a monoid object for dependent context
> extension; and the projection `𝒯̃₀ ⟶ 𝒯₀`, from telescopes-with-a-filling, is a
> representable natural transformation: a **natural model** whose types are
> telescopes and whose comprehension is context extension.

Everything is stated over a fixed carrier `C`.

```
§1        the raw layer, recalled
§2        the one change to an existing definition: equations become boundaries
§3        the notation the rest of the note uses
§§4–5     preparation: the boundary of an expression, equality of boundaries
§§6–7     the judgements: well-formedness, and equality
§8        what must be proved about them
§§9–13    the categorical structure
§14       what exists in the repository, and what has to be written
```

## 1. The raw layer

Recalled from `Carrier.lean`, `Expr.lean`, `Renaming.lean`, `Subst.lean`,
`SlotPath.lean`; nothing here is new.

**1.1 Arities.** `C.Arity` is a monoid, written multiplicatively as `Γ ⋈ Δ`
with unit `1`. For arities `Γ` and `α`, `Γ ∋ α` is the well-ordered type of
**slots** of `Γ` with binding arity `α`. Every slot `x : Γ ∋ α` splits its
arity,

```
C.before x ⋈ C.after x = Γ          (C.factor x)
```

and `C.inclusion x : C.before x →ʳ Γ` is the resulting renaming. The unit has no
slots; the slots of `Γ ⋈ Δ` are those of `Γ` followed by those of `Δ`, injected
by `C.inl` and `C.inr`, with `C.copair` the induced case analysis. `C.subWf` is
the well-founded relation making a slot's binding arity smaller than the arity it
sits in; the recursions of §7 are on it.

**1.2 Renamings.** `Γ →ʳ Δ := ∀ ⦃α⦄, Γ ∋ α → Δ ∋ α`, with identity `𝟙ʳ`,
composition `∘ʳ`, extension `ρ ⇑ʳ Ξ : Γ ⋈ Ξ →ʳ Δ ⋈ Ξ` and
`Renaming.prefixed Ω ρ : Ω ⋈ Γ →ʳ Ω ⋈ Δ`.

Renamings and substitutions are written the same way round — the arity whose
slots are consumed comes first (1.4) — so

```
Subst.ofRenaming :  (Γ →ʳ Δ)  →  (Γ ⇒ Δ)
```

and both act covariantly: `⟦ρ⟧ʳ : Expr Γ → Expr Δ`,
`Bd.rename ρ : Bd Γ → Bd Δ` (2.2), and likewise on decorations (3.2).


**1.3 Expressions.**

```
inductive Expr : C.Arity → Type
  | ap {Γ α} (x : Γ ∋ α) (args : ∀ ⦃Λ⦄, α ∋ Λ → Expr (Γ ⋈ Λ)) : Expr Γ
```

`Expr` is generated freely: it mentions no boundary and no judgement.
`Expr.η : Γ ∋ α → Expr (Γ ⋈ α)` is the fully applied slot.

**1.4 Substitutions.**

```lean
abbrev Subst (Γ Δ : C.Arity) := ∀ ⦃α⦄, Γ ∋ α → Expr (Δ ⋈ α)

infixr:25 " ⇒ " => Subst
```

*A substitution `σ : Γ ⇒ Δ` supplies, for each slot of `Γ`, an expression over
`Δ` extended by that slot's binding arity.* The orientation is 1.2's.

```
Subst.id Γ  : Γ ⇒ Γ
Subst.act   : Δ ⇒ (Γ ⋈ Ξ) → (Φ : C.Arity) → Expr (Γ ⋈ Δ ⋈ Φ) → Expr (Γ ⋈ Ξ ⋈ Φ)
Subst.comp  : Δ ⇒ (Γ ⋈ Θ) → Θ ⇒ (Γ ⋈ Ξ) → Δ ⇒ (Γ ⋈ Ξ)
Subst.lift  : Γ ⇒ Δ → (Φ : C.Arity) → (Γ ⋈ Φ) ⇒ (Δ ⋈ Φ)
```

**1.5 Slot paths.** `SlotPath Δ Φ α` addresses a slot reached from `Δ` by entering
binding arities: `Δ` is where the walk starts, `α` is the binding arity of the slot
reached, and `Φ` accumulates what precedes it on the way.

```lean
inductive SlotPath : C.Arity → C.Arity → C.Arity → Type where
  | here   {Δ α}     (x : Δ ∋ α)                      : SlotPath Δ (C.before x) α
  | nested {Δ β Φ α} (x : Δ ∋ β) (p : SlotPath β Φ α) : SlotPath Δ (C.before x ⋈ Φ) α
```

`nested` accumulates on the **left**, `C.before x ⋈ Φ`, because the entries
preceding `x` are in scope for everything inside `x`'s binding arity and not
conversely. That order is what makes `Decoration.nested` typecheck (3.1, 3.4):
entering a slot moves `C.before x` out of the accumulator and into the base.

**1.6 The monoid laws on arities are definitional.** `1 ⋈ Γ = Γ`, `Γ ⋈ 1 = Γ` and
`Γ ⋈ Δ ⋈ Φ = Γ ⋈ (Δ ⋈ Φ)` hold by `rfl`, `⋈` being multiplication in
`Submonoid (Function.End A)`, and they transport `∋`, `Bd` and `dTel` silently: a
term of one type is accepted at the other with no cast. Arities are therefore
written below in whichever form reads best — with `1` omitted in 3.3, 3.4, 3.5 and
4.2, and at replacement `1` in 3.6.

The carrier's own equations are propositional and do need transports: `C.factor`,
and `C.before_inl` as used by `SlotPath.inl` (3.4). The `Boundary.cast` and
`dTel.castBase` of the existing code are applied to the monoid laws instead, where
they are not needed for types to agree; 11.3's unitors and associator are morphisms
in `ArityMod` and are separate content.

**1.7 The raw layer is a relative monad.** The first of the note's three
structural statements; 3.8 and 13.2 are the others, and 13.5 lists them. `SyntaxMonad C` packages 1.3
and 1.4 as a relative monad over the **slot functor**:

```
J Γ  =  (α ↦ Γ ∋ α)             T Γ  =  (α ↦ Expr (Γ ⋈ α))
```

with unit `Expr.η`, Kleisli extension `Subst.act`, and the three laws `act_η`,
`act_id`, `act_comp` (`MonadLaws.lean`). A Kleisli morphism *is* a substitution —
`syntaxKleisliHomEquiv` is `Equiv.refl` — so

```
Kl(SyntaxMonad C)     objects: raw arities        Hom Γ Ξ = Γ ⇒ Ξ
```

## 2. Boundaries

**Definition 2.1.** The boundary of a slot over `Ω`:

```lean
inductive Bd (Ω : C.Arity) : Type where
  | sort
  | of (S : Expr Ω)
  | eq (l r : Expr Ω)
```

Intuitively, `sort` declares its slot to be a sort and `of S` declares it to be an
object of the sort `S`; these two are `Typing/Boundary.lean`'s as it stands. The
change is `eq l r`, which declares its slot to **assert the equation** `l ≈ r`.

The binding telescope of such an equational slot is what the equation is quantified over — so an axiom's telescope of metavariables is the
binding telescope of an equational slot, and an equational slot inside a binding
arity is an equational premise.

**2.2 Actions.** Renaming and substitution act on a boundary componentwise:

```lean
Bd.rename (ρ : Γ →ʳ Δ) : Bd Γ → Bd Δ
  | .sort => .sort   | .of S => .of (⟦ρ⟧ʳ S)   | .eq l r => .eq (⟦ρ⟧ʳ l) (⟦ρ⟧ʳ r)

Bd.act (σ : Δ ⇒ (Γ ⋈ Ξ)) (Φ) : Bd (Γ ⋈ Δ ⋈ Φ) → Bd (Γ ⋈ Ξ ⋈ Φ)
  | .sort => .sort   | .of S => .of (σ.act Φ S)  | .eq l r => .eq (σ.act Φ l) (σ.act Φ r)
```

**Lemma 2.3.** Both preserve the constructor. So whether a boundary is an `eq`
is an invariant property which we denote by `Bd.isEq`.

## 3. Ambients, telescopes, and the notation of this note

§§3.1–3.2 recall `Decoration.lean`, `DecorationModule.lean` and
`DecoratedTelescopeMonoid.lean`. §§3.3–3.7 are the notation everything after this
section is written in, and 3.4 is its only new construction; 3.8 is the second of
the three structural statements.

### 3.1 Decorations

```lean
abbrev Decoration (Ω Δ : C.Arity) : Type :=
  ∀ ⦃Φ α⦄, SlotPath Δ Φ α → Bd (Ω ⋈ Φ ⋈ α)

structure dTel (Ω : C.Arity) where
  arity : C.Arity
  decoration : Decoration Ω arity
```

A decoration assigns a boundary to every immediate and every nested slot.

### 3.2 Existing operations

```lean
Decoration.rename     (ρ : Γ →ʳ Δ)              : Decoration Γ Ξ → Decoration Δ Ξ
Decoration.substitute (σ : Γ ⇒ (S ⋈ Δ))         : Decoration (S ⋈ Γ ⋈ Φ) Ξ → Decoration (S ⋈ Δ ⋈ Φ) Ξ
Decoration.empty                                : Decoration Ω 1
Decoration.concatenate                          : Decoration Ω Γ → Decoration (Ω ⋈ Γ) Δ → Decoration Ω (Γ ⋈ Δ)
```

with the same for `dTel`, satisfying `concatenate_empty_left`,
`concatenate_empty_right`, `concatenate_assoc`, `act_empty`, `act_concatenate`,
`act_id`, `act_comp`.

**All three actions leave the underlying arity alone**, and do so definitionally:
each is `arity := Θ.arity` with only the decoration transported, so
`|Θ.rename ρ| = |Θ|`, `|Θ.act σ| = |Θ|` and `|Θ.substitute σ| = |Θ|` need no
transport. Every comparison of arities across an action below appeals to this.

### 3.3 Ambients

**Definition.** An **ambient** is a `Ξ : dTel 1`; the empty ambient is denoted `𝟙`.
`Ambient` abbreviates `dTel 1`.

**Variables.** In §§1–2 every capital is a bare arity. From here on:

```
Ξ, Ψ, Γ     ambients                      Ω, α, Λ, Φ  bare arities
Θ, Δ        telescopes over the ambient   x, z, q, w  slots
σ, θ, τ, κ  substitutions                 e, l, r, S  expressions
β           a boundary
```

A definition still binds whatever data it introduces; the convention only fixes
what an unadorned letter is.

**Notation.** `|Θ|` is the `arity` field of a `Θ : dTel Ω` (3.1), in particular `|Ξ|`
for an ambient. **Erasures are written out**: an ambient or a telescope appearing
where an arity is expected carries its `|−|`, as in `Expr |Ξ|`, `dTel |Ξ|`,
`|Θ| ∋ α`, `|Θ| ⇒ |Ξ|`. That last is read the same way between two ambients,
`|Ψ| ⇒ |Ξ|`, and out of a bare arity, `α ⇒ |Ξ|` (4.1): substitution is between
arities, the decorations playing no part in its type.

Concatenation of decorated telescopes is

```lean
dTel.concatenate {Ω : C.Arity} (Θ : dTel Ω) (Δ : dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ|)) : dTel Ω
  arity      := |Θ| ⋈ |Δ|
  decoration := Decoration.concatenate Θ.decoration Δ.decoration
```

— the second factor written over the base *extended by the first*. The note's
`Ξ ⋈ Θ` is this at `Ω := 1`: for an ambient `Ξ : dTel 1` and a `Θ : dTel |Ξ|` it
gives the ambient

```
Ξ ⋈ Θ  :  dTel 1          with   |Ξ ⋈ Θ| = |Ξ| ⋈ |Θ|
```

reading `Θ` as a `dTel (1 ⋈ |Ξ|)` by `1 ⋈ |Ξ| = |Ξ|` (1.6). The `⋈` on arities is
1.1's. Iterated extension associates to the left, so `Ξ ⋈ Θ ⋈ Δ` requires
`Δ : dTel |Ξ ⋈ Θ|`.

**Why the base is an ambient and not a bare arity.** Nothing in 3.4–3.7 reads
`Ξ`'s decoration: every type there mentions `Ξ` only through `|Ξ|`, and each
operation is one of 3.2's arity-indexed ones. A bare arity would do, and the
displays would then be literally the Lean signatures. The decoration is first read
at 4.2, in `Ξ.boundary x`.

The base is an ambient because of what `⋈` must *produce*. §6 writes results of it
to the left of a turnstile, as in `Ξ ⋈ κ ⋆ Θ.binding z ⊢ σ z : …`, and `Wf_e` and
`Eq` are indexed by a decoration — they cannot be stated over a bare arity. Were
3.4–3.7 arity-level, `Ξ ⋈ Θ.before z` would be an arity only, and §6 would have to
build the decorated concatenation separately and prove the two agree. Taking the
base to be an ambient here makes the `⋈` that types the projections the same one
§6 indexes a judgement by.

### 3.4 The three projections of a slot

Let `Ω : C.Arity`, `Θ : dTel Ω` and `z : |Θ| ∋ Λ`. Three things are attached to `z`:

```
Θ.before   z  :  dTel Ω                                    the entries of Θ preceding z
Θ.binding  z  :  dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z|)                   the entries z binds
Θ.boundary z  :  Bd   (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z| ⋈ |Θ.binding z|)   what z is declared to be
```

Each line is written over the base extended by the erasures of the previous ones,
so the third reads: *`z`'s boundary is written over the base, the entries preceding
`z`, and the entries `z` binds.* The two erasures are
`|Θ.before z| = C.before z` and `|Θ.binding z| = Λ`. Nothing here needs the base to be
decorated, so `Ω` is a bare arity throughout 3.4–3.7; for an ambient `Ξ` take
`Ω := |Ξ|`, and for the ambient's own slots — `Ξ.before x`, `Ξ.binding x`,
`Ξ.boundary x` as read in 4.2, 6.3 and 6.4(2) — take `Ω := 1`.

In terms of the code:

- `Θ.binding z := ⟨Λ, Θ.decoration.nested z⟩` and
  `Θ.boundary z := Θ.decoration.boundary z`, both already available;
- `Θ.before z` is restriction along `C.factor z`:

  ```lean
  SlotPath.inl : SlotPath Γ Φ α → SlotPath (Γ ⋈ Δ) Φ α
    | .here x     => C.before_inl x ▸ .here (C.inl x)
    | .nested x p => C.before_inl x ▸ .nested (C.inl x) p

  Decoration.restrict (D : Decoration Ω (Γ ⋈ Δ)) : Decoration Ω Γ  :=  D ∘ SlotPath.inl
  ```

**The running ambient.** Fix, once, for the examples in 3.4, 3.6 and 6.2:

```
Ξ₀  =  [ ty : sort,  tm : [A : ty] sort,  ℕ : ty,  Vec : [n : tm ℕ] ty,  3 : tm ℕ ]
```

**Example.** Over `Ξ₀`, with

```
Θ = [ A : ty,  B : [x : tm A] ty,  a : tm A,  b : tm (B a) ]
```

| `z` | `Θ.before z` | `Θ.binding z` | `Θ.boundary z` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A` | `[ ]` | `[ ]` | `of ty` |
| `B` | `[ A : ty ]` | `[ x : tm A ]` | `of ty` |
| `a` | `[ A, B ]` | `[ ]` | `of (tm A)` |
| `b` | `[ A, B, a ]` | `[ ]` | `of (tm (B a))` |

In the second column a bare name abbreviates that entry together with its
declaration; `Θ.before z` carries the declarations, not just the names.

### 3.5 Weakening `⇑`

Let `Ω : C.Arity`, `Θ : dTel Ω` and `z : |Θ| ∋ Λ`.
The entries preceding `z` sit inside `Θ`, by the renaming

```
Renaming.prefixed Ω (C.inclusion z)  :  Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z| →ʳ Ω ⋈ |Θ|
```

Write `⇑` for its action. Three operations:

```
⇑ : Expr (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z|)  → Expr (Ω ⋈ |Θ|)      Renaming.act
⇑ : Bd   (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z|)  → Bd   (Ω ⋈ |Θ|)      Bd.rename
⇑ : dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z|)  → dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ|)      Decoration.rename
```

and, extending the renaming by `⇑ʳ` through a suffix `Φ : C.Arity` (1.2), three
more:

```
⇑ : Expr (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z| ⋈ Φ) → Expr (Ω ⋈ |Θ| ⋈ Φ)
⇑ : Bd   (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z| ⋈ Φ) → Bd   (Ω ⋈ |Θ| ⋈ Φ)
⇑ : dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z| ⋈ Φ) → dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ| ⋈ Φ)
```

of which the first three are the case `Φ = 1`. The suffix is a raw arity, as it is
in 1.4's `Subst.act` and 2.2's `Bd.act`, and is untouched.

The same symbol is used for the **base weakening**, which reads an ambient as a
telescope over a base. Along the unique `1 →ʳ Ω`, with `Ψ : dTel 1` and `|Ψ|` as
the suffix:

```
⇑ : Expr 1 → Expr Ω          ⇑ : Expr |Ψ| → Expr (Ω ⋈ |Ψ|)
⇑ : Bd   1 → Bd   Ω          ⇑ : Bd   |Ψ| → Bd   (Ω ⋈ |Ψ|)
⇑ : dTel 1 → dTel Ω          ⇑ : dTel |Ψ| → dTel (Ω ⋈ |Ψ|)
```

the right column using `1 ⋈ |Ψ| = |Ψ|` (1.6), and again `|⇑Ψ| = |Ψ|`.

Of these the note uses `dTel 1 → dTel Ω` at 6.5's `⇑Ψ` and 9.2's `⇑Γ`, and
`Expr |Ψ| → Expr (Ω ⋈ |Ψ|)` at 3.6's `σ ⋆ e`; the `Θ.before z` form above is the
`⇑` of 4.2, 6.3 and 6.4(2).

### 3.6 Instantiation `⋆`

Let `Ω, α : C.Arity` and `σ : α ⇒ Ω`. Three operations, written `σ ⋆ −`:

```
σ ⋆ −  :  Expr (Ω ⋈ α) → Expr Ω        Subst.act
σ ⋆ −  :  Bd   (Ω ⋈ α) → Bd   Ω        Bd.instantiate
σ ⋆ −  :  dTel (Ω ⋈ α) → dTel Ω        Decoration.substitute
```

and, for a suffix `Φ : C.Arity`, three more:

```
σ ⋆ −  :  Expr (Ω ⋈ α ⋈ Φ) → Expr (Ω ⋈ Φ)
σ ⋆ −  :  Bd   (Ω ⋈ α ⋈ Φ) → Bd   (Ω ⋈ Φ)
σ ⋆ −  :  dTel (Ω ⋈ α ⋈ Φ) → dTel (Ω ⋈ Φ)
```

of which the first three are the case `Φ = 1`, `Bd.instantiate` being that case of
`Bd.act` in the code. Neither the consumed block nor the suffix is decorated: `⋆`
is arity-level, as in 1.4, 2.2 and 3.2. The decorated reading is the instance
`α := |Δ|` for a `Δ : dTel Ω`, which is how §§4–7 use it; 4.1's `args : α ⇒ |Ξ|`
uses it at a block that is a binding arity.

*`σ ⋆ −` consumes the middle block, replacing each slot of `α` by the expression
`σ` gives it; the prefix and the suffix survive.*

```
   Ω      ⋈       α      ⋈      Φ
 prefix        middle        suffix
survives      consumed      survives
```

**Example.** Over `Ξ₀` (3.4), so `Ω := |Ξ₀|`, with the block `α := |Δ|` for
`Δ = [ A : ty, B : [x : tm A] ty ]`, `σ = ( A ↦ ℕ, B ↦ [x] Vec x )` and the suffix
`Φ := |[ x : tm A ]|`:

```
σ ⋆ (tm A)            =  tm ℕ
σ ⋆ [ x : tm A ]      =  [ x : tm ℕ ]
σ ⋆ (of (tm (B x)))   =  of (tm (Vec x))       over  Ξ₀ ⋈ [ x : tm ℕ ]
```

the second line being the `dTel` operation applied to the telescope whose arity is
the suffix of the third.

**Reading the fillers.** By 1.4 the expression `σ` gives to a slot of binding arity
`α` lives in the arity extended by `α`. For `A`, whose binding arity is `1`, that is
`Expr |Ξ₀|`, so `σ A = ℕ`. For `B`, whose binding arity has one slot, the filler must
live in `|Ξ₀|` extended by that slot, and `[x] Vec x` denotes

```
the expression  Vec x   over   |Ξ₀| ⋈ (B's binding arity),   with  x  naming the added slot
```

`[x]` is not an operator and not a λ: there is no binder in the term, only an
expression in a larger arity, and `[x]` tells the reader which name refers to the
added slot.

So brackets are doing three different jobs here. `[ A : ty, B : … ]` is a
telescope. The `[x : tm A]` inside `B`'s declaration is `B`'s binding telescope,
and its `: tm A` is a decoration. `[x] Vec x` is a filler, and carries no
decoration at all — the boundary at `x` comes from the telescope being filled,
never from `σ`.

**Restriction, and instantiating a declaration.** For `Ω : C.Arity`, `Θ : dTel Ω`,
`σ : |Θ| ⇒ Ω` and `z : |Θ| ∋ Λ`:

```
σ ↾ z  :=  σ ∘ C.inclusion z   :   |Θ.before z| ⇒ Ω

(σ ↾ z) ⋆ Θ.binding  z  :  dTel Ω
(σ ↾ z) ⋆ Θ.boundary z  :  Bd   (Ω ⋈ |Θ.binding z|)
```

`σ ↾ z` is the components of `σ` preceding `z`. The last two are `z`'s declaration
with those entries replaced by their fillers, taking `α := |Θ.before z|` and
`Φ := |Θ.binding z|` above.

**Substitutions between two ambients.** Later sections also want to write `σ ⋆ e`
when `σ : |Ψ| ⇒ |Ξ|` runs between two *ambients* and `e : Expr |Ψ|`. The
substitution is already an instance of the above, at `Ω := |Ξ|` and `α := |Ψ|`; its
argument is not, living in `Expr |Ψ|` where `Expr (|Ξ| ⋈ |Ψ|)` is wanted. The base
weakening of 3.5 repairs that, `⇑e : Expr (|Ξ| ⋈ |Ψ|)`, so we write

```
σ ⋆ e  :=  σ ⋆ ⇑e   :  Expr |Ξ|
```

and the same on `Bd |Ψ|` and on `dTel |Ψ|`. This is the reading of `σ ⋆ −` at
6.4(3), 6.5, 10.2 and 13.1.

### 3.7 The two operations, together

For `Ω : C.Arity`, `Θ : dTel Ω` and `z : |Θ| ∋ Λ` as in 3.5, and for
`σ : |Θ.before z| ⇒ Ω` — which is `σ' ↾ z` when a filling `σ'` of all of `Θ` is at
hand — every type in §§4–7 is built by applying `⇑` or `⋆` to one of the three
projections of 3.4. Both act on the same block,
`Θ.before z`: `⇑` replaces it by the whole of `Θ`, `⋆` consumes it. Neither touches
the projection itself.

```
Θ.binding z        : dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z|)
⇑ (Θ.binding z)    : dTel (Ω ⋈ |Θ|)
σ ⋆ (Θ.binding z)  : dTel Ω

Θ.boundary z       : Bd (Ω ⋈ |Θ.before z| ⋈ |Θ.binding z|)
⇑ (Θ.boundary z)   : Bd (Ω ⋈ |Θ|          ⋈ |Θ.binding z|)
σ ⋆ (Θ.boundary z) : Bd (Ω                ⋈ |Θ.binding z|)
```

### 3.8 Raw telescopes are a monoid

The second of the three structural statements, and the one already formalized
(`DecoratedTelescopeMonoid.lean`). It is about the **raw, unquotiented**
telescopes; no judgement occurs in it.

`DTel` is a module over `SyntaxMonad C`, that is, a functor `Kl(T) ⥤ Type` sending
an arity to the decorated telescopes over it and acting by `Decoration.act`. Every
element has an underlying arity, and that arity is invariant under the action, so
`DTel` is **arity-shaped**: an object of

```
ArityMod T  :=  Over (arityConst T)          arityConst T : Kl(T) ⥤ Type,  Ω ↦ C.Arity
```

`ArityMod T` carries a tensor which extends the base by the shape of the first
component,

```
(M ⊗ N) Ω  :=  Σ (m : M Ω), N (Ω ⋈ |m|)
```

— dependent sequencing, not a product — with unit `I` the constant `PUnit` of
shape `1`. Then:

> **`DTel` is a monoid object in `(ArityMod T, ⊗, I)`**, with unit
> `dTel.empty` and multiplication `concatenate`, the three laws
> being `concatenate_empty_left`, `concatenate_empty_right` and
> `concatenate_assoc`.

Two features to carry forward. The multiplication is *dependent*: `concatenate`
takes `Γ : DTel Ω` and `Δ : DTel (Ω ⋈ |Γ|)`, so the second factor is written over
the base extended by the first — which is why the tensor is what it is. And the
base is extended by a **raw arity**, because `Kl(T)`'s objects are raw arities and
a raw arity is all that is needed to extend one. That second feature is the one
that fails in the well-formed layer; 13.5 says what replaces it.

## 4. The boundary of an expression

**4.1 The arguments of an application are a substitution.** Let `Ξ` be an ambient.
An expression over it is an `ap x args`, with

```
x     :  |Ξ| ∋ α
args  :  ∀ ⦃Λ⦄, α ∋ Λ → Expr (|Ξ| ⋈ Λ)   =   α ⇒ |Ξ|        by 1.3 and 1.4
```

By 3.4 the telescope `Ξ.binding x` has underlying arity `α`, and renaming leaves
an underlying arity alone (3.2), so `|⇑(Ξ.binding x)| = α` and

```
args  :  |⇑(Ξ.binding x)| ⇒ |Ξ|
```

So the arguments of an application are a substitution filling the telescope its
head binds. This is used again at 6.3 and 9.2.

**Definition 4.2 (the boundary of an expression).** For an ambient `Ξ`,

```
Ξ.boundaryOf : Expr |Ξ| → Bd |Ξ|
Ξ.boundaryOf (ap x args) := args ⋆ ⇑(Ξ.boundary x)
```

Here 3.4 and 3.5 are used at `Ω := 1`, with `Θ := Ξ`: the declaration
`Ξ.boundary x : Bd (|Ξ.before x| ⋈ |Ξ.binding x|)` is weakened to
`Bd (|Ξ| ⋈ |Ξ.binding x|)` and then instantiated by `args`, whose block is
`α`. Both erasures are `α`. The weakening is 3.5's suffixed group at
`Φ := α`, the instantiation 3.6's plain group; in the code the two are
`Bd.rename (Renaming.prefixed 1 (C.inclusion x) ⇑ʳ α)` and `Bd.instantiate args`,
composing with no transport.

Two properties. It is not a recursion: only the head is inspected. And it is
total — a decoration gives a boundary to *every* slot, so `boundaryOf` extends it
to every expression.

How `boundaryOf` moves under `⇑` and `⋆` is 8.1(1) and 8.1(2).

## 5. Equality of boundaries

**Definition 5.1.** For an ambient `Ξ` and a relation `≈` on `Expr |Ξ|`, extend it
to `Bd |Ξ|`:

```
sort ≈ sort          of S ≈ of S'  iff  S ≈ S'          eq l r ≈ eq l' r'  iff  l ≈ l' and r ≈ r'
```

and no other pairs. It is an equivalence relation whenever `≈` is. The relation on
expressions is 6.4; this section only lifts it, and is used at 6.1 and in §7.

`Ξ ⊢ − ≈ −` is written for all three of expressions (6.4), boundaries (here) and
telescopes (7.3); which is meant is fixed by the arguments.

## 6. The judgements

Three relations, all indexed by an arbitrary ambient, defined by one
simultaneous induction:

```
Wf_e : (Ξ : Ambient) → Expr |Ξ| → Prop                         written  Ξ ⊢ e
Eq   : (Ξ : Ambient) → Expr |Ξ| → Expr |Ξ| → Prop              written  Ξ ⊢ e ≈ e'
Wf_s : (Ξ : Ambient) → (Θ : dTel |Ξ|) → (|Θ| ⇒ |Ξ|) → Prop     written  Ξ ⊢ σ : Θ
```

Read 6.2–6.4 as the clauses of one definition: the occurrences of `Wf_e` and `Eq`
in 6.2, and of `Wf_s` in 6.3 and 6.4(3), are simultaneous. `Wf_s` is not itself
inductive — 6.2 is a universally quantified conjunction of `Wf_e` and `Eq` premises.
How to encode that in Lean is 6.7.


**Notation 6.1.** For an ambient `Ξ`, an `e : Expr |Ξ|` and a `β : Bd |Ξ|` write

```
Ξ ⊢ e : β     for     Ξ ⊢ e   and   Ξ ⊢ Ξ.boundaryOf e ≈ β .
```

This is an abbreviation and not a primitive judgement: it is what "`e` is a thing
of boundary `β`" means here. Two consequences of taking it as derived rather than
primitive:

- *One judgement, not two.* There is no checking judgement to define, and no
  rules relating it to `Wf_e`; the boundary is computed by 4.2 rather than carried
  as an index.
- *Conversion needs no rule.* In 6.1 the boundary `β` occurs exactly once, as the
  right-hand argument of an `≈`, and the conjunct `Ξ ⊢ e` does not mention it at
  all. So `β ↦ (Ξ ⊢ e : β)` is `≈`-invariant by construction. Conversion
  is thus neither a rule nor an admissibility theorem.

**Definition 6.2 (filling).** Let `Ξ` be an ambient, `Θ : dTel |Ξ|` and `σ : |Θ| ⇒ |Ξ|`
— one expression over `Ξ` for each slot of `Θ`, a slot of binding arity `α`
receiving an expression over `Ξ ⋈ α` (1.4). We say that *`σ`
fills `Θ` over `Ξ`*, written  `Ξ ⊢ σ : Θ`,  when for every `z : |Θ| ∋ Λ`:

```
(σ↾z) ⋆ Θ.boundary z = .eq l r  :   Ξ ⋈ (σ↾z) ⋆ Θ.binding z ⊢ l ≈ r
otherwise                       :   Ξ ⋈ (σ↾z) ⋆ Θ.binding z ⊢ σ z : (σ↾z) ⋆ Θ.boundary z
```

*An equational slot imposes its equation; every other slot must be filled by a
thing of the boundary declared for it.* The branch is legitimate by Lemma 2.3:
whether the boundary is an `eq` does not depend on `σ`.

**Reading 6.2.** The judgement is `Ξ ⊢ σ : Θ` in the ordinary sense — *`σ` is a
well-typed instantiation of the interface `Θ` in the context `Ξ`*; algebraically,
*`σ` is a model of `Θ` in `Ξ`*.


Take `Θ` to be the telescope of 3.4's example over the ambient `Ξ₀` of 3.4, and let
`e : Expr |Ξ₀|` be arbitrary. For

```
σ = ( A ↦ ℕ,  B ↦ [x] Vec x,  a ↦ 3,  b ↦ e )
```

walk the slots in precedence order, writing `κ := σ ↾ z` at each:

| `z` | `κ ⋆ Θ.binding z` | `κ ⋆ Θ.boundary z` | premise |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A` | `[ ]` | `ty` | `Ξ₀ ⊢ ℕ : ty` |
| `B` | `[ x : tm ℕ ]` | `ty` | `Ξ₀ ⋈ [x : tm ℕ] ⊢ [x] Vec x : ty` |
| `a` | `[ ]` | `tm ℕ` | `Ξ₀ ⊢ 3 : tm ℕ` |
| `b` | `[ ]` | `tm (Vec 3)` | `Ξ₀ ⊢ e : tm (Vec 3)` |

The first three premises hold by the declarations of `Ξ₀`; the fourth is the
condition `e` must satisfy for `σ` to fill `Θ`.

Three things are happening, and Definition 6.2 is their conjunction:

1. **A slot's boundary mentions the slots before it.** `Θ.boundary b = tm (B a)`
   lives in `Bd |Ξ ⋈ Θ.before b|`, with `Θ.before b = [A, B, a]` — and in `Bd |Ξ|`
   is where the filler's boundary `Ξ.boundaryOf (σ b)` lives, so the two are not
   yet of the same type. Applying `κ = σ ↾ b`, which sends `B ↦ [x] Vec x` and
   `a ↦ 3`, turns `B a` into `Vec 3`, so the required boundary is
   `κ ⋆ Θ.boundary b = tm (Vec 3)`, now in `Bd |Ξ|`.
2. **The ambient grows at a binding slot.** `Θ.binding B` is `[x : tm A]`,
   written over `Θ.before B = [A]`; instantiated it is
   `κ ⋆ Θ.binding B = [x : tm ℕ]`, and `B`'s filler — an expression over
   `Ξ ⋈ κ ⋆ Θ.binding B` by 1.4 — is checked there. This is the only place higher
   rank enters; `κ ⋆ Θ.binding z` is a whole telescope, so its own entries may
   bind in turn and no further clause is needed.
3. **At an equational slot the premise constrains `σ`'s earlier components, not
   `σ z`.** If `κ ⋆ Θ.boundary z = .eq l r` then `l, r : Expr |Ξ ⋈ κ ⋆ Θ.binding z|`
   by 2.1, and the premise `Ξ ⋈ κ ⋆ Θ.binding z ⊢ l ≈ r` mentions `σ` only through
   `κ`, hence only through the components at slots preceding `z`. The filler `σ z`
   does not occur in it and is therefore unconstrained. The premise is stated in
   the extended ambient because that is where `l` and `r` live; the entries `z`
   binds remain free slots there, so the premise asserts the equation for every
   instance of them.

**Definition 6.3 (well-formedness).** One clause, for an ambient `Ξ`, a slot
`x : |Ξ| ∋ α` and an `args : α ⇒ |Ξ|`:

```
   ¬ (Ξ.boundary x).isEq        Ξ ⊢ args : ⇑(Ξ.binding x)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                     Ξ ⊢ ap x args
```

*An expression is well formed exactly when no head of it is an equational slot
and, at every head, its arguments fill the telescope that head binds.*

The first premise is what keeps an equational slot from being referred to. `Expr`
is free over the raw arity and cannot see decorations (1.3), so for an equational
`w` the raw expression `ap w args` exists like any other. Without the premise:

```
Ξ ⊢ ap w args                          would hold
Ξ.boundaryOf (ap w args)             is an eq-boundary                    (4.2)
    — so §5 keeps it out of every data-slot filler and every `of S` boundary,
    — but 7.1 would still accept it as a side of an `eq` boundary.
```

With the premise such expressions are never well formed, so 7.1's `Ξ_z ⊢ l` and
`Ξ_z ⊢ r` exclude them there too.

The recursion lives in 6.2, in the premise
`Ξ ⋈ κ ⋆ Θ.binding z ⊢ σ z : κ ⋆ Θ.boundary z`, which unfolds by 6.1 to a `⊢` of
`σ z` in the extended ambient.

**Definition 6.4 (equality).** `Ξ ⊢ e ≈ e'` is generated by three clauses:

1. **equivalence** — at each ambient: symmetry, transitivity, and reflexivity for
   well-formed expressions,

   ```
      Ξ ⊢ e
   ────────────
    Ξ ⊢ e ≈ e
   ```

2. **hypothesis** — for an ambient `Ξ` and a `q : |Ξ| ∋ Λ` whose declared boundary
   is `Ξ.boundary q = .eq l r`,

   ```
    Ξ ⋈ ⇑(Ξ.binding q) ⊢ ⇑l          Ξ ⋈ ⇑(Ξ.binding q) ⊢ ⇑r
   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
             Ξ ⋈ ⇑(Ξ.binding q)  ⊢  ⇑l ≈ ⇑r
   ```

   *An equational slot of the ambient asserts its equation, with the entries it
   binds left as free slots.* For a well-formed ambient the two premises are
   automatic — 7.1 checks exactly them, one ambient earlier — and they carry over
   by weakening. They are stated because §6 is defined at every ambient, well
   formed or not (7.2); 6.4c is what goes wrong without them.

3. **substitution** — for ambients `Ψ, Ξ`, expressions `e, e' : Expr |Ψ|` and
   substitutions `σ, θ : |Ψ| ⇒ |Ξ|` with
   `Ξ ⊢ σ : ⇑Ψ` and `Ξ ⊢ θ : ⇑Ψ` — well-formedness of a substitution, 6.5 — that
   agree up to `≈` at every non-equational `z : |Ψ| ∋ Λ`, meaning
   `Ξ ⋈ (σ ↾ z) ⋆ (⇑Ψ).binding z ⊢ σ z ≈ θ z`:

   ```
      Ψ ⊢ e ≈ e'
   ──────────────────────
      Ξ ⊢ σ ⋆ e ≈ θ ⋆ e'
   ```

   The ambient of the premises is built from `σ`, not from `θ`; 6.4d says why that
   is harmless.

   Equational `z` are excluded although the hypothesis is well-typed at them —
   `σ z` and `θ z` exist at every slot — because 6.2 constrains no filler at an
   equational slot: requiring agreement there would ask for what nothing declares.
   9.1's `∼` omits them for the same reason, and 12.2 needs `∼` to imply this
   hypothesis.

**6.4a The characterization.** Each clause is a closure condition with a standard
name, so `≈` is pinned down by:

> `≈` is the **least family of equivalence relations on well-formed expressions
> that contains the ambient's declared equations and for which well-formed
> substitution is a congruence** — that is, for which `⋆` descends to `≈`-classes
> in both of its arguments, the expression and the substitution.

**6.4b Congruence is a theorem.** For an ambient `Ξ`, a slot `x : |Ξ| ∋ α` and
`args, args' : α ⇒ |Ξ|` with `ap x args` and `ap x args'` well formed, writing
`Δ := ⇑(Ξ.binding x)`:

```
for every non-equational z : |Δ| ∋ Λ :   Ξ ⋈ (args ↾ z) ⋆ Δ.binding z ⊢ args z ≈ args' z
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                         Ξ ⊢ ap x args ≈ ap x args'
```

This holds because an application is a substitution instance
of a variable,

```
ap x args  =  args ⋆ Expr.η x            Expr.η x : Expr |Ξ ⋈ Δ|
```

whose head lies in the prefix block: `act_left` keeps it, and `act_middle`,
`act_η_right`, `act_inst_id` reduce each argument to `args i`. This is a new lemma
of the raw layer — `act_η`'s computation one level down.

Clause 3 then applies to `Expr.η x ≈ Expr.η x` along `⟨η, args⟩` and `⟨η, args'⟩`, the
substitutions `|Ξ ⋈ Δ| ⇒ |Ξ|` that are the identity on `Ξ` and the argument family on
`Δ`. They are well formed by the variable rule and 6.3's premise, and they agree up
to `≈` at every non-equational slot: on `Ξ` by reflexivity, on `Δ` by the premises.

Two remarks. Clause 3 is used here with the same expression on both sides and
different substitutions, so a single-substitution clause would not do (6.4a). And
sorts are heads, so this covers congruence for sorts as well: no theory declares a
congruence rule.

**6.4c Why clauses 1 and 2 carry well-formedness premises.** Without them the
relation collapses. Let `Ψ` have an equational slot `q` binding nothing, and let
`F, G : Expr |Ψ|` be arbitrary:

```
reflexivity      Ψ ⊢ Expr.η q ≈ Expr.η q
σ := Subst.id,   θ := Subst.id except θ q := F
                 both well formed — 6.2 asks nothing of a filler at an equational
                 slot, only that the equation hold — and equal at every
                 non-equational slot
clause 3         Ψ ⊢ Expr.η q ≈ F         and likewise    Ψ ⊢ Expr.η q ≈ G
clause 1         Ψ ⊢ F ≈ G
```

The premise blocks the first line, since `Expr.η q` is not well formed for
equational `q` (6.3). Clause 2 would otherwise supply that line by itself: for an
equational `p` and a declaration `Ψ.boundary q = .eq (Expr.η p) (Expr.η p)` — raw
decorations being unconstrained — a premise-free clause 2 gives
`Ψ ⊢ Expr.η p ≈ Expr.η p`, and the three steps above run unchanged at `p`. Hence
the premises on both clauses.

Clause 3 needs no such premise, preserving well-formedness by 8.1(3). So every
pair related by `≈` has well-formed sides — 8.1(7) — which is what makes 6.4a exact
rather than hedged: `≈` is a family of equivalence relations *on the well-formed
expressions*, with nothing said about the rest.

**6.4d Why clause 3's asymmetry is harmless.** Clause 3 states its premises in the
ambient built from `σ`. The clause with `θ` in that position generates the same
relation, and the argument uses only symmetry:

```
given         Ξ ⋈ (θ ↾ z) ⋆ (⇑Ψ).binding z ⊢ σ z ≈ θ z       and   Ψ ⊢ e ≈ e'
clause 1      Ξ ⋈ (θ ↾ z) ⋆ (⇑Ψ).binding z ⊢ θ z ≈ σ z       and   Ψ ⊢ e' ≈ e
              — these are clause 3's own premises for the pair (θ, σ),
                whose left-hand family is the one the ambient is built from
clause 3      Ξ ⊢ θ ⋆ e' ≈ σ ⋆ e
clause 1      Ξ ⊢ σ ⋆ e  ≈ θ ⋆ e'
```

No judgement is moved between ambients: the rule is re-instantiated at the swapped
pair, and it is the rule that builds the ambient. The two ambients *are* `≈`-equal
when `Ψ` is well formed, by 8.1(9); the argument above does not use that, which is
why clause 3 needs no well-formedness hypothesis on `Ψ`. Where the two ambients
cannot be avoided is the symmetry of `∼` (9.1), which has no rule to
re-instantiate — that is the remark on 8.1(11).

**6.5 Well-formed substitutions.** For ambients `Ψ, Ξ`, a raw `σ : |Ψ| ⇒ |Ξ|` is
**well formed** when `Ξ ⊢ σ : ⇑Ψ`, where `⇑Ψ : dTel |Ξ|` reads `Ψ` — a telescope
over the empty ambient — as a telescope over `Ξ` (3.5). No separate notation is
needed: this is 6.2 at `Θ = ⇑Ψ`.

**6.6 Why the definition is legitimate.** `Wf_e`, `Eq` and `Wf_s` are
**inductive**: the least family closed under the clauses. Nothing has to decrease
and no well-founded recursion has to be justified; the only requirement is that
the operator be monotone, which for a Lean `inductive` is strict positivity of the
occurrences of `Wf_e` and `Eq` in the premises. That holds — every occurrence is a
premise, none under a negation or to the left of an arrow.

Positivity rather than decrease is exactly what is available, as clause 3 shows:
it concludes about `σ ⋆ e` from a premise about `e`, and `σ ⋆ e` is in general
*larger* than `e`. There is no recursion on the size of expressions to be had here,
and none is needed. Likewise 6.2 is a `∀` over the slots of `Θ`, not a recursion on
`Θ` — arities being an abstract monoid, there is no induction on a telescope to do —
which is what lets it stand as a premise.

Finally, the layering. `Expr` is defined first and freely (1.3), so `Wf_e` and `Eq`
are predicates on a type that already exists: nothing is inductive–recursive, and
`Expr` never depends on a judgement.

**6.7 Encoding in Lean.** `Wf_s` is a `∀` of `Wf_e` and `Eq` premises, so it is
either inlined at its two use sites — 6.3 and 6.4(3) — with `Wf_s` defined
afterwards, or made a third member of the mutual block with one constructor. Under
the first choice `Ξ ⊢ ap x args` unfolds to a *conjunction*,
`¬ (Ξ.boundary x).isEq ∧ Wf_s Ξ (⇑(Ξ.binding x)) args`, and not to `Wf_s` alone.
The case distinction in 6.2 is a pattern match on the boundary, not a test for
equality with `.eq`; as two constructors guarded by `Bd.isEq` and its
negation it avoids a `Prop`-valued `match` inside a constructor type. Nothing in
§§6–13 depends on which encoding is chosen.

## 7. Well-formed telescopes, and equality of telescopes

Both are recursions on nesting, well-founded by `C.subWf`; they use §6 but are
not mutual with it. The turnstile is §6's, overloaded on its right-hand argument:
`Ξ ⊢ Θ` carries a telescope where `Ξ ⊢ e` carries an expression, and the letter
tells which (3.3).

**Definition 7.1.** For an ambient `Ξ` and `Θ : dTel |Ξ|`, say `Θ` is **well formed
over `Ξ`**, written `Ξ ⊢ Θ` and named `Wf_t`, when for every `z : |Θ| ∋ Λ`, writing

```
Ξ_z  :=  Ξ ⋈ Θ.before z ⋈ Θ.binding z
```

- `Ξ ⋈ Θ.before z ⊢ Θ.binding z`, and
- one of

  ```
  Θ.boundary z = .sort
  Θ.boundary z = .of S      with   Ξ_z ⊢ S : .sort
  Θ.boundary z = .eq l r    with   Ξ_z ⊢ l,  Ξ_z ⊢ r,  Ξ_z ⊢ Ξ_z.boundaryOf l ≈ Ξ_z.boundaryOf r
  ```

An ambient `Ξ` is **well formed** when `𝟙 ⊢ Ξ`.

The three conditions at an `eq` entry make the equation a *statement*; 7.1 does not
require `l ≈ r`, the entry being what declares it. 6.2's equational branch does
require it, and 8.1(7) returns those same three conditions from that one premise —
which is why 6.2 asks for nothing further there, while its non-equational branch
carries a boundary comparison through 6.1.

**Restriction.** No hypothesis `Ξ ⊢ Θ.before z` has to be added: it follows. For a
`w` inside `Θ.before z`, `C.before_inl` makes the entries preceding `w` there the
ones preceding it in `Θ`, and `Decoration.restrict` keeps its declaration (3.4), so
the condition imposed at `w` by `Ξ ⊢ Θ.before z` is the one `Ξ ⊢ Θ` already
imposes. This is needed: with the first bullet and concatenation (10.4) it is what
makes `Ξ_z` a *well-formed* ambient, which every theorem of §8 presupposes of the
ambients it names.

**7.2 Why this is not circular.** For every ambient, well formed or not, §6
defines `Wf_e` and `Eq`. Definition 7.1 is a separate recursion whose premises at
`z` are stated over `Ξ_z`, which is built from the entries strictly preceding `z`
and the entries `z` binds — never from `z` itself. So an equation cannot be used
to justify itself, and equations declared earlier *are* available when a later
entry is checked. Both directions matter:

- an equational entry whose two sides have different computed boundaries is well
  formed only if an earlier equation makes them `≈`;
- an entry `of S` whose `S` is well formed only under an earlier equation is
  accepted exactly when that equation precedes it, as in

  ```
  [ A : ty,  A' : ty,  q : (A ≡ A'),  B : [x : tm A] ty,  a : tm A',  z : tm (B a) ]
  ```

  where the entry at `z` is well formed only because `B a` is, which needs
  `boundaryOf a = tm A' ≈ tm A`, supplied by `q` alone.


**Definition 7.3 (equality of telescopes).** For an ambient `Ξ` and
`Θ, Θ' : dTel |Ξ|`, say `Ξ ⊢ Θ ≈ Θ'` when `|Θ| = |Θ'|` — strictly — and for every
`z : |Θ| ∋ Λ`, with `Ξ_z` as in 7.1,

- `Ξ ⋈ Θ.before z ⊢ Θ.binding z ≈ Θ'.binding z`, and
- `Ξ_z ⊢ Θ.boundary z ≈ Θ'.boundary z`.

The ambient used is the one built from `Θ`; that symmetry and transitivity
nevertheless hold is 8.1(11). Underlying arities are compared strictly, since
`Expr` is indexed by them.

## 8. What must be proved

Theorems about §§6–7, presupposed by §§9–13. Each is universally quantified over
the ambients, telescopes, slots, substitutions and expressions named in it, by the
convention of 3.3, and well-formedness of the ambient is a hypothesis of all of
them.

**8.1** Cited below as 8.1(1)–8.1(11).

*How `boundaryOf` moves.*

1. `(Ξ ⋈ Θ).boundaryOf (⇑e) = ⇑(Ξ.boundaryOf e)`, strictly.
2. `Ξ.boundaryOf (σ ⋆ e) ≈ σ ⋆ (Ψ.boundaryOf e)`, for a well-formed `σ : |Ψ| ⇒ |Ξ|`.

*Stability of the judgements.*

3. `Wf_e` is preserved by well-formed substitution.
4. `Wf_s` is preserved by well-formed substitution: from `Γ ⊢ τ : Θ` and
   `σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ`, `Ξ ⊢ (σ ⋆ τ) : σ ⋆ Θ`. At `Θ = ⇑Γ'` this says that well-formed
   substitutions are closed under `≫`; `Subst.id` is well formed by (5).
5. For non-equational `x`, `Ξ ⊢ Expr.η x`; and `Ξ.boundaryOf (Expr.η x)` is
   `x`'s declared boundary.
6. Congruence: the rule of 6.4b.
7. Presuppositions: if `Ξ ⊢ e ≈ e'` then `Ξ ⊢ e` and `Ξ ⊢ e'`, and
   `Ξ ⊢ Ξ.boundaryOf e ≈ Ξ.boundaryOf e'`. The induction is over the clauses of
   6.4: clauses 1 and 2 carry well-formedness as premises, and clause 3 preserves
   it by (3).

*The telescope level.*

8. `Wf_t` is preserved by `⇑`: from `𝟙 ⊢ Γ`, `Ξ ⊢ ⇑Γ`.
9. `Wf_t` and `≈` on telescopes are preserved by well-formed substitution. And, in
   the other argument: for one `Θ` with `Ξ ⊢ Θ`, two well-formed `σ, θ` that agree
   up to `≈` at every non-equational slot give `σ ⋆ Θ ≈ θ ⋆ Θ`. This is clause
   6.4(3) applied to the decorations — well formed because `Ξ ⊢ Θ` — lifted through
   the suffix each decoration carries by `Subst.lift`.
10. `Wf_e`, `Eq` and `Wf_t` are invariant under replacing the ambient by an
    `≈`-equal one (7.3).
11. `≈` on telescopes (7.3), `∼` on fillings (9.1), and 13.1's heterogeneous
    comparison of fillings of two `≈`-equal telescopes are equivalence relations.

Four remarks on the list.

(2) holds only up to `≈` because the head of `e` is replaced by `σ` at that head,
whose declared boundary is itself only `≈` the transport of the original's.

(3), (4) and (7) are one simultaneous induction, whose argument at a head runs
over the slots of its binding arity in precedence order, since a slot's
instantiated boundary depends on the fillers preceding it.

(6) needs (5) and two lemmas of the raw layer, neither mentioning a judgement:
`ap x args = args ⋆ Expr.η x`, by `act_left` and `act_middle` as in 6.4b, and
`⟨η, args⟩ ⋆ e = args ⋆ e`, whose nearest existing relatives are `act_idOfη`
(`Instantiation.lean`) and `act_interchange.aux` (`Interchange.lean`). Where (6) is
used inside the induction of (3), (4), (7), well-formedness must already be
available at the point of use.

(11) is *not* immediate from (10). Symmetry of `∼` compares `τ z` and `τ' z` in the
two ambients `Ξ ⋈ (τ ↾ z) ⋆ Θ.binding z` and `Ξ ⋈ (τ' ↾ z) ⋆ Θ.binding z`, so it
needs those to be `≈`-equal, which is (9)'s second half, given agreement at the
slots *preceding* `z`. The proof is therefore an induction over the slots in
precedence order, as in (3), (4), (7) — and the same for `≈` on telescopes.

13.1's relation varies the telescope as well as the filling, so there *both* halves
of (9) are used and chained by transitivity of `≈` on telescopes; its reflexivity
needs `Wf_e` of each component, which is 6.2's premise at that slot read through
6.1.

**8.2** (10) and (11) are what make §§9–13 well posed; (4) makes `Ctx` a category;
(8) makes 9.2's `Filling Ξ (⇑Γ)` defined at all. The identity substitution's
obligation at an equational slot `q` is discharged by clause 6.4(2) at `q` itself.

## 9. Contexts, and fillings

From here on everything is **setoid-first**: each construction is given on
representatives together with a proof that it respects an equivalence, and no
quotient is taken anywhere. Which quotients would be harmless and which would not
is 10.5.

**Definition 9.1 (fillings).** For an ambient `Ξ` and `Θ : dTel |Ξ|` with `Ξ ⊢ Θ`,

```
Filling Ξ Θ  :=  { τ : |Θ| ⇒ |Ξ|  //  Ξ ⊢ τ : Θ }
```

carrying the relation

```
τ ∼ τ'   iff   Ξ ⋈ (τ ↾ z) ⋆ Θ.binding z ⊢ τ z ≈ τ' z    at every non-equational z : |Θ| ∋ Λ
```

Components at equational slots are not compared: nothing constrains them (6.2)
and nothing depends on them (6.4(3)). That `∼` is an equivalence is 8.1(11) — not
a triviality, the ambients built from `τ` and from `τ'` being different.

**Definition 9.2 (contexts).** `Ctx` has the well-formed ambients as objects and

```
Hom Ξ Γ  :=  Filling Ξ (⇑Γ)                         well defined by 8.1(8)
```

as hom-setoids, with identity `Subst.id` and composition `≫` (1.4), both
respecting `∼` by 8.1(4). So `Ctx` is a category enriched in setoids; a morphism
`σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ` assigns an expression over `Ξ` to each slot of `Γ`.

That a hom is a filling is 4.1 and 6.5 again: filling a head's binding telescope
and giving a context morphism are one judgement. §13 turns on it.

The empty ambient is **terminal**: `Hom Ξ 𝟙 = Filling Ξ (⇑𝟙)` has exactly one
element, the empty family. So the reading of §13 as a category with families has
its base case.

**9.3 Direction.** `σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ` is what type theory writes `Ξ ⊢ σ : Γ`, so `Ctx` is
the category of contexts in the usual sense, `𝒯₀` below is **contravariant**, and
the raw Kleisli category of 1.7, whose hom `Γ ⟶ Ξ` is `|Γ| ⇒ |Ξ|`, runs the other way.

**9.4 Relation to the raw layer.** `Filling Ξ Θ` is a subtype of the raw
substitutions, and `∼` is coarser than equality on it. So the passage from
`Kl(SyntaxMonad C)` to `Ctx` is *restrict, then coarsen*: the first step is
injective, the second is not, and there is no functor `Ctx ⟶ Kl(T)ᵒᵖ` recovering
the raw substitution — a morphism of `Ctx` is a `∼`-class, and a class has no
underlying substitution. What survives is that every structure map below is
induced by a raw one.

## 10. Telescopes, and context extension

**Definition 10.1.** For `Γ : Ctx`,

```
𝒯₀ Γ  :=  { Θ : dTel |Γ|  //  Γ ⊢ Θ }        with the equivalence  Γ ⊢ − ≈ −   (7.3)
```

**Definition 10.2 (action).** For `Ξ, Γ : Ctx` and `σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ`, `σ ⋆ − : 𝒯₀ Γ → 𝒯₀ Ξ`
is 3.6. It
preserves `Wf_t` and `≈` and respects `∼` in `σ`, by 8.1(9); functoriality is
`act_id` and `act_comp`. So

```
𝒯₀ : Ctxᵒᵖ ⥤ Setoid
```

and `σ ⋆ Θ` is what type theory writes `Θ[σ]`.

**10.3 Why the index moves.** `𝒯₀` is not the constant functor on arities. In

```
Γ = [ A : ty, B : ty, f : [z : tm A] tm A, g : [z : tm B] tm B ]
```

`f` and `g` have the same binding arity and different decorations, and a
well-formed substitution must send them to expressions well formed under
different hypotheses. There is no single set of well-formed expressions of a
given raw arity for both to land in. This is the first parting of ways with 3.8,
whose shape was a raw arity and whose index was constant; 13.5 draws the
consequence.

**Definition 10.4 (extension).** For `Γ : Ctx` and `Θ : 𝒯₀ Γ`,

```
Γ ⋈ Θ  :  Ctx            3.3, with  𝟙 ⊢ Γ ⋈ Θ  from  𝟙 ⊢ Γ  and  Γ ⊢ Θ
p Γ Θ  :  Γ ⋈ Θ ⟶ Γ      Subst.ofRenaming C.inl,  well formed by 8.1(5)
```

and for `Ξ : Ctx` and `σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ` the lifted substitution and its square

```
Subst.lift σ |Θ| : Ξ ⋈ σ ⋆ Θ ⟶ Γ ⋈ Θ
(Subst.lift σ |Θ|) ≫ p Γ Θ  =  p Ξ (σ ⋆ Θ) ≫ σ
```

**10.5 Why nothing is quotiented here.** `Γ ⋈ Θ` is an *object* of `Ctx`, so a
quotient `𝒯 Γ := 𝒯₀ Γ / ≈` would leave it undefined: `Γ ⊢ Θ ≈ Θ'` gives distinct
ambients, and 8.1(10) supplies only an isomorphism `Γ ⋈ Θ ≅ Γ ⋈ Θ'`, never an
equality. Setoid-first keeps 10.4 literally true, and the isomorphism becomes a
theorem about `⋈` rather than a defect of it.

Quotienting the *hom-setoids* would be harmless — they are ordinary setoids and
nothing is indexed by a morphism. It is `𝒯₀` that must not be quotiented, because
`Γ ⋈ −` takes its elements to *objects*.

## 11. The monoid of telescopes

The well-formed counterpart of 3.8.

**Definition 11.1.** A **telescope-shaped presheaf** is an `M : Ctxᵒᵖ ⥤ Setoid`
with a natural `shape : M ⟶ 𝒯₀`. They form

```
TelFam  :=  Over (𝒯₀ : Ctxᵒᵖ ⥤ Setoid)
```

the analogue of `ArityMod = Over (arityConst)` of 3.8, with the constant module
of raw arities replaced by `𝒯₀`.

**Definition 11.2 (tensor and unit).** For `M, N : TelFam`, `Γ, Ξ : Ctx`,
`σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ`, `m : M Γ` and `n : N (Γ ⋈ shape m)`:

```
(M ⊗ N) Γ           :=  Σ (m : M Γ), N (Γ ⋈ shape m)
(M ⊗ N) σ (m, n)    :=  (M σ m, N (Subst.lift σ |shape m|) n)
shape (m, n)        :=  shape m ⋈ shape n

I Γ  :=  PUnit,   shape _ := 𝟙
```

Well typed by 10.4's square together with naturality of `shape`, which gives
`shape (M σ m) = σ ⋆ shape m`. Dependent sequencing, not a product.

**11.3** `(TelFam, ⊗, I)` is monoidal. The unitors and associator are
`concatenate_empty_left`, `concatenate_empty_right` and `concatenate_assoc`; the
triangle and pentagon are not automatic and have to be proved, as they are in
`ArityModuleTensor.lean`, where the comparisons are transports along equalities of
arities (1.6) and the diagrams follow by proof irrelevance. Setoid-first, the same
proofs are needed, together with respect for `≈`.

**Theorem 11.4.** `𝒯₀`, with `shape := 𝟙`, is a **monoid object** in
`(TelFam, ⊗, I)`:

```
unit            η : I ⟶ 𝒯₀                Γ ↦ 𝟙
multiplication  μ : 𝒯₀ ⊗ 𝒯₀ ⟶ 𝒯₀         (Θ, Λ) ↦ Θ ⋈ Λ
```

The three laws are the three `concatenate` lemmas; naturality of `μ` is
`act_concatenate`, of `η` is `act_empty`.

**11.5** The unit, the multiplication and the laws are literally 3.8's operations.
The whole content of 11.4 is that each survives the cut to `Wf_t` and respects the
coarsening by `≈` — which is 8.1(9).

## 12. Expressions and boundaries

**Definition 12.1.** For `Γ : Ctx`,

```
ℰ₀ Γ  :=  { e : Expr |Γ|  //  Γ ⊢ e }         with  Γ ⊢ − ≈ −
ℬ₀ Γ  :=  Bd |Γ|                            with  Γ ⊢ − ≈ −   (§5)
```

**12.2 Both are presheaves.** For `Ξ, Γ : Ctx` and `σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ` the action on either
is `σ ⋆ −`,
respecting the equivalences for two separate reasons: 8.1(3) gives
`Ξ ⊢ σ ⋆ e`, and clause 6.4(3) — stated for `σ ∼ θ` and `e ≈ e'` at once —
makes the action respect `∼` in the substitution and `≈` in the expression
together. Functoriality is `act_id` and `act_comp`. So
`ℰ₀, ℬ₀ : Ctxᵒᵖ ⥤ Setoid`, the second by the same argument on the components of a
boundary (2.2).

**12.3 The boundary map.** `boundaryOf` respects `≈` by 8.1(7) and is natural by
8.1(2). So

```
boundaryOf : ℰ₀ ⟶ ℬ₀
```

and the map to keep in view is this one — `ℰ₀` as an object of `PSh(Ctx)/ℬ₀`,
equivalently a presheaf on the total category of `ℬ₀`, with

```
ℰ₀ Γ β  :=  { e : ℰ₀ Γ  //  Γ ⊢ boundaryOf e ≈ β }
                                   σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ  ⟹  ℰ₀ Γ β → ℰ₀ Ξ (σ ⋆ β)
```

the fibre being taken up to `≈`, `ℬ₀` being a setoid. The fibre over a boundary
fixed at one `Γ` is not a subpresheaf of `ℰ₀`, the boundary moving under
substitution. It has the shape of a natural model without being one, which is
13.4.

## 13. The natural model

The third structural statement, and the one the note is for. A **natural model**
in Awodey's sense is a representable natural transformation of presheaves: `p : Ṫ ⟶ T`
such that every pullback of `p` along a representable is representable. The
comprehension of a category with families is exactly such a `p`, with `T` the
types and the fibres the terms.

**Definition 13.1.** For `Γ : Ctx`,

```
𝒯̃₀ Γ  :=  Σ (Θ : 𝒯₀ Γ), Filling Γ Θ          q  :=  fst : 𝒯̃₀ ⟶ 𝒯₀
```

— a telescope together with a filling of it — with the **dependent** equivalence

```
(Θ, τ) ≈ (Θ', τ')    iff    Γ ⊢ Θ ≈ Θ'    and    τ ∼ τ'
```

Note that `τ` and `τ'` fill *different* telescopes, so the second conjunct is not
literally 9.1's `∼`: it is the heterogeneous version, comparing `τ z` with `τ' z`
at every non-equational `z` in the ambient built from `Θ` and `τ`. It typechecks
because 7.3 compares underlying arities strictly, so `Θ` and `Θ'` have the same
slots; that it is an equivalence, and independent of which side builds the ambient,
is 8.1(10) and 8.1(11).

For `Ξ : Ctx` and `σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ`, put `σ ⋆ (Θ, τ) := (σ ⋆ Θ, σ ⋆ τ)`, the filling acted on
componentwise — which is `Subst.comp τ σ`, since
`Subst.comp τ σ = fun z => σ ⋆ (τ z)`. That this fills `σ ⋆ Θ` is 8.1(4), and that
it respects the equivalence is 8.1(9). So `𝒯̃₀ : Ctxᵒᵖ ⥤ Setoid`, and `q` is
natural because `σ ⋆ −` acts on the two components independently.

**Theorem 13.2 (representability).** For `Γ : Ctx` and `Θ : 𝒯₀ Γ` there is an
isomorphism of setoids, natural in `Ξ`,

```
Hom Ξ (Γ ⋈ Θ)   ≅   Σ (σ : Hom Ξ Γ), Filling Ξ (σ ⋆ Θ)
```

equivalently, the square

```
                    (⇑Θ, ν)
        y(Γ ⋈ Θ) ──────────→ 𝒯̃₀
            │                 │
   y(p Γ Θ) │                 │ q
            ↓         Θ       ↓
          y Γ ───────────────→ 𝒯₀
```

is a pullback in `PSh(Ctx)`. The horizontals are the elements the two presheaves
receive by Yoneda: `Θ` below, and above the **generic** telescope-with-filling

```
⇑Θ : 𝒯₀ (Γ ⋈ Θ)                          Θ read over the extended ambient (3.5)
ν  := Subst.ofRenaming C.inr             each slot of Θ filled by itself
```

`ν` fills `⇑Θ` by 8.1(5), its obligations at equational slots discharged by clause
6.4(2) as in 8.2, and it is the mirror of `p Γ Θ = Subst.ofRenaming C.inl`. Both are
`Subst.ofRenaming` — `fun ⦃_⦄ x => Expr.η (ρ x)`, the identity substitution read
along `ρ` — so `p` and `ν` are the two injections of the `C.copair` that 13.2's
proof splits along, and `Expr.η` on its own is `Subst.id`.

The square commutes because `q` reads the first component and `p ⋆ Θ = ⇑Θ`, acting
by `Subst.ofRenaming ρ` being renaming along `ρ` (`act_ofRenaming`).

So **`q` is representable, and `𝒯̃₀ ⟶ 𝒯₀` is a natural model** whose types are
telescopes and whose comprehension is context extension. Read elementwise, the
pullback property says that every telescope-with-filling over `Θ` is a substitution
instance of `(⇑Θ, ν)`, in exactly one way.

*Proof.* By 9.2 a morphism `Ξ ⟶ Γ ⋈ Θ` is a filling of `⇑(Γ ⋈ Θ)` over `Ξ`, that
is, by 6.2, one component per slot of `Γ ⋈ Θ`. The slots of `Γ ⋈ Θ` are those of
`Γ` followed by those of `Θ` (1.1), so **splitting** the family along `C.copair`
gives a `σ` on `Γ`'s slots and a `τ` on `Θ`'s. That `σ` is a morphism `Ξ ⟶ Γ` is
its half of the premises; that `τ` fills `σ ⋆ Θ` is the other half, since a
declaration of `Θ` mentions `Γ`'s slots and 6.2 instantiates them by the
components already given, which are exactly `σ`. **Pairing** is the converse, and
the two are mutually inverse because a family over `Γ ⋈ Θ` is determined by its
two halves. Both respect `∼`, which is defined slotwise, and naturality in `Ξ`
holds because both sides act by `σ ⋆ −` and splitting is slotwise. ∎

One point about the right-hand side, since the pullback is computed in setoids.
Its fibre at `σ` is really `Σ (Θ' : 𝒯₀ Ξ) (_ : Ξ ⊢ Θ' ≈ σ ⋆ Θ), Filling Ξ Θ'` —
the `𝒯₀`-component matches only up to `≈`. Transporting a filling along a
telescope equality, by 8.1(10), identifies that with `Filling Ξ (σ ⋆ Θ)`, which is
how the theorem writes it.

**13.3 Sanity check: equations give a subobject.** If every entry of `Θ` is
equational, `Filling Ξ (σ ⋆ Θ)` has at most one element up to `∼` — its components
are unconstrained and uncompared — and it is inhabited exactly when the
instantiated equations hold. So 13.2 makes `p Γ Θ` **monic**: extending by
equations alone is a subobject inclusion, which is what "imposing an equation" ought
to mean.

**13.4 Why `boundaryOf : ℰ₀ ⟶ ℬ₀` is *not* representable.** It has the shape of a
natural model — 12.3 — with `ℬ₀` as types and the fibres as terms. Representability
would ask, for `Γ` and `β : ℬ₀ Γ`, that

```
(y Γ ×_{ℬ₀} ℰ₀)(Ξ)  =  { (σ, e)  |  σ : Ξ ⟶ Γ,  Ξ ⊢ boundaryOf e ≈ σ ⋆ β }
```

be represented by an object `Γ.β` extending `Γ` by one slot of boundary `β`. No
such object exists in general: an entry of a telescope is a **binding arity
together with a boundary** (3.4), and 1.1 provides no one-slot arity to build one
from. `boundaryOf` is representable precisely when `Ctx` has extension by a single
boundary, which is when the carrier supplies a one-slot, nothing-binding arity;
then `ℬ₀` embeds into `𝒯₀` as the rank-0 single entries and 13.2 subsumes it.

So `𝒯₀`, not `ℬ₀`, is the presheaf of types. Telescopic extension is primitive and
extension by a single type is a special case that a general carrier need not admit.

**13.5 The layers together.** The three structural statements are starred; 11.4 is
listed alongside them because 13.2 is stated over its tensor.

```
★ 1.7    Expr            a relative monad over the slot functor    raw
★ 3.8    DTel            a monoid in ArityMod                      raw
  11.4   𝒯₀              a monoid in TelFam                        well formed, up to ≈
★ 13.2   q : 𝒯̃₀ ⟶ 𝒯₀     a natural model                           well formed, up to ≈
```
